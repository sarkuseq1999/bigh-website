The Science · Berkeley

The brain ages where its power is made.

NuriCell is built on a formula known in the research literature as CellGen — developed by Dr. Jian Kang Liu, a biochemist who did his landmark work in the Division of Biochemistry & Molecular Biology at the University of California, Berkeley.

“The science started in a lab at Berkeley. NuriCell finishes the sentence in a capsule.”

The mitochondrial theory of brain aging is simple. Mitochondria are the power plants inside every cell. They make the energy — ATP — that thinking, remembering, and focusing all depend on.

With age, mitochondria decay. Oxidative damage builds up in their lipids, proteins, RNA and DNA. They make less energy and leak more damage — and that decline tracks closely with the memory loss and mental fatigue of aging.

Working alongside the renowned scientist Dr. Bruce Ames, Dr. Liu published a landmark finding in PNAS in 2002: feeding two mitochondrial nutrients — acetyl-L-carnitine and R-alpha-lipoic acid — to old rats partially reversed age-associated brain mitochondrial decay and improved their performance on memory tasks. The combination worked better than either nutrient alone. Electron-microscope imaging showed aged mitochondria structurally restored toward a younger state.

NuriCell takes that research and builds it into a daily capsule: the two studied mitochondrial nutrients, plus two more that support the same energy-and-memory system.

How It Works

Decay is the problem. Fuel is the answer.

Three steps describe how brain energy fades — and where NuriCell steps into the cycle.

Mitochondria decay

Energy and memory slip

Less ATP means less of the fuel that recall, focus and learning depend on. The afternoon fog and the slower word-finding follow from the same source.
