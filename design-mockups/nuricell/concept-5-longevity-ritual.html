The Science

The mitochondrial
theory of brain aging.

Inside every brain cell sit mitochondria — tiny power plants that make the energy thinking, remembering, and focusing all depend on. With age, they decay. Oxidative damage builds up in their lipids, proteins, RNA and DNA. They make less energy and leak more damage, and that decline tracks with the memory loss and mental fatigue of getting older.

Dr. Liu's landmark finding, published in PNAS in 2002, was quietly remarkable. Feeding two mitochondrial nutrients — acetyl-L-carnitine and R-alpha-lipoic acid — to old rats partially reversed that decay and improved their performance on memory tasks. The two worked better together than either did alone. Electron-microscope imaging showed aged mitochondria structurally restored toward a younger state.
