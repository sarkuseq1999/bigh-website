The Science

A lab at Berkeley, and a theory about how the brain ages.

NuriCell is built on a formula known in the research literature as CellGen — work led by biochemist Dr. Jian Kang Liu.

Mitochondria are the power plants inside every cell. They make the energy — ATP — that thinking, remembering, and focusing all depend on. With age, mitochondria decay: oxidative damage builds up in their lipids, proteins, RNA and DNA. They make less energy and leak more damage. This slow decline tracks closely with the memory loss and mental fatigue so many people notice as the years pass. The idea has a name — the mitochondrial theory of brain aging.

Dr. Jian Kang Liu did his landmark work in the Division of Biochemistry & Molecular Biology at the University of California, Berkeley, alongside the renowned scientist Dr. Bruce Ames. Their question was direct: if mitochondria decay with age, can the right nutrients help them recover? 1

The science started in a lab at Berkeley. NuriCell finishes the sentence in a capsule.

Liu's landmark finding, published in PNAS in 2002, was striking. Feeding two mitochondrial nutrients — acetyl-L-carnitine and R-alpha-lipoic acid — to old rats partially reversed age-associated brain mitochondrial decay and improved their performance on memory tasks.2 The combination worked better than either nutrient alone. Electron-microscope imaging showed aged mitochondria structurally restored toward a younger state.3

NuriCell takes that research and builds it into a daily capsule: the two studied mitochondrial nutrients, plus two more that support the same energy-and-memory system.
