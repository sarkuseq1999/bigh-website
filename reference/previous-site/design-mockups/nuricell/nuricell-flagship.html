01 / The Problem

Your mind runs on power plants. They're aging.

Inside every brain cell sit mitochondria — microscopic engines that produce the energy thinking, remembering, and focusing all depend on. When they run clean, the mind feels quick.

With age, that machinery decays. Oxidative damage accumulates in their lipids, proteins, RNA and DNA. They make less energy and leak more damage — a decline that tracks closely with the memory loss and mental fatigue of getting older.

It started in a lab at Berkeley.

NuriCell is built on a formula known in the research literature as CellGen, developed by biochemist Dr. Jian Kang Liu in the Division of Biochemistry & Molecular Biology at the University of California, Berkeley.

The landmark finding, published in PNAS.

Dr. Liu fed two mitochondrial nutrients — acetyl-L-carnitine and R-alpha-lipoic acid — to old rats. The result: age-associated brain mitochondrial decay was partially reversed, and the animals improved on memory tasks.
