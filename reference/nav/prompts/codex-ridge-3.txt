A very long, very low horizon of California's golden summer hills, seen from far away, painted loose in ink and thin watercolour on plain white paper. It runs as one thin band straight across the whole width of the picture, edge to edge, centred top to bottom, and it is very flat: the band is only about one fourteenth of the picture's height, mostly a gentle low ridge with three or four soft rounded rises along its length, never a mountain. The ridge top is one fine, quick line of warm sepia-brown ink drawn with a small round brush, unbroken, with a faint natural waver; under it a pale wash of straw gold and warm ochre that fades down into the white paper within a short distance, like dry summer grass. Bare hills only: no trees, no bushes, no rocks, no fences. Light and airy, much white paper, soft wet edges, a few granulation speckles in the wash. Everything else is pure white: no sky colour, no sun, no clouds, no foreground, no people, no animals, no buildings, no text, no letters, no signature, no seal, no stamp, no frame, no border, no shadows, no paper texture. Flat even lighting, photographed straight from above, wide landscape picture.
