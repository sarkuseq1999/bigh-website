STYLE: A painting for a California brand's website, in loose ink and thin watercolour on plain warm white paper. Use the attached paintings only as the reference for the brush hand (do not paint what they show): the same painter, the same few wet washes with soft bleeding edges, a few dry-brush streaks, very few strokes, lots of bare paper. The more it looks like a painting, the better. Not photo-real, not a detailed botanical drawing, not a storybook illustration, no pen outlines. Mostly soft grey ink, with only a thin touch of the colours named below.

It is in the spirit of an American naturalist's field collection, NOT an Asian ink painting: no scroll, no pagoda, no bamboo, no twisted pine on a rock, no characters, no seal or stamp.

COMPOSITION: Square, 1024 x 1024. Four plant specimens, laid out like a botanist's collection in a loose 2 by 2 arrangement, each one with wide bare paper around it so the four never touch. No sheet edges, no table, no frame, no background, no shadows beyond a faint pale wash under each.

THE FOUR SPECIMENS:
- Top left: a sprig of the Brazilian green-propolis shrub (Baccharis dracunculifolia): one slender stem with many narrow, soft green leaves and a few tiny resinous green-amber buds at the tips. One honeybee rests on it, painted in three or four quick washes (amber and grey), plainly a honeybee.
- Top right: one fresh turmeric root, a knobbly hand of finger-like rhizomes in warm ochre-orange, one end broken to show its deep orange inside, with one long lance-shaped green leaf rising from it.
- Bottom left: a small hanging cluster of dark blue-purple wine grapes on a short brown stem, with one grape leaf in thin grey-green.
- Bottom right: one brown pine cone lying beside a curled, rough piece of reddish-brown pine bark.

Each specimen has one small blank paper tag tied to it with a loose grey string; the tags are bare paper with NO writing at all.

INK FIRST, COLOUR SECOND (the painter's client asked for this): the first try came out as a colourful, careful watercolour illustration, like a greeting card. Paint it instead mostly in grey and black sumi ink washes, like the attached oak: the stems, leaves, grape leaf, pine cone and bark in shades of grey ink with dry-brush edges. Colour only as thin, pale touches laid over the ink: a pale ochre wash on the turmeric with its broken end a muted, earthy orange (not bright); a thin grey-violet on the grapes; a pale grey-green on the leaves; a warm brown-grey on the bark. Fewer strokes than you think: the pine cone is a few dark wet dabs, not every scale; the grapes are a few overlapping wet circles bleeding into each other.

HOW LOOSE: paint each specimen in just a few wet washes, the way the attached paintings are painted. Big soft shapes that bleed at the edge, nothing outlined, no fine veins, no speckle or grain. If a part can be left to bare paper, leave it. From across a room each of the four must still read at once: propolis sprig with a bee, turmeric root, grapes, pine cone and bark.

NEVER: no people, no hands, no text, letters, numbers or writing anywhere (not on the tags either), no signature, no lab glassware, no pills or capsules, no powder, no jars or bottles, no red or rust clustered dots, no glow, no halo, no sparkle, no gold.
