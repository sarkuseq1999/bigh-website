ORDER: Low tide at a rocky tide pool on the California coast (like Point Lobos or Monterey), on a sunny late afternoon. A wide landscape painting.

In front, a clear, shallow tide pool among warm grey granite rocks with bright green sea lettuce. In the water: three or four orange-ochre and soft purple sea stars, a few pale green sea anemones, a scatter of small snail shells. The water is pale sea-blue and clear, a few soft reflections.

At the pool's edge, a little left of centre, two people crouch close together, seen from the side: a curious older woman (silver hair, a straw sun hat, a soft coral sweater) holds a small round hand lens just above the water, leaning in to look; beside her a park naturalist (a sage-green shirt, a wide-brimmed ranger hat) kneels and points into the pool at one of the sea stars, showing her. They are small in the painting (together about a quarter of its height), painted loosely in a few strokes.

Behind them: a few more rocks, then the calm Pacific, and a headland with a few Monterey cypress trees pale in the haze. A soft pale sky with lots of white paper. The colours are soft and sunny: pale sea-blue, sand, warm granite grey, sea-lettuce green, the sea stars' ochre and purple, the woman's coral.

Composition: the pool and the two people fill the lower two thirds, the light sky and the far headland the top; the whole painting fades into white paper at its edges.
