Two reference paintings are attached. The FIRST is our red-crowned crane at rest: copy its brush hand exactly (the same painter, the same sumi ink, the same bold few strokes: one loaded, wet, jet-black neck stroke; a white body that is bare unpainted paper held by one calligraphic contour line that swells, thins and breaks, with only two or three broad pale grey wash strokes under the wing; heavy jet-black tail plumes ending in split dry-brush tips; each leg one quick thin dark stroke with a small knot at the joint; the small crown a tiny touch of gold leaf, never red). The SECOND is the California park sign the crane will stand beside on our website: copy its loose watercolour sage-green grass tufts and its plain warm cream paper exactly. Do NOT paint the sign, its posts or its poppy.

Paint exactly ONE crane, standing still on two thin legs in a low tuft of sage-green grass (the same loose grass blades as at the sign's posts, with the same faint pale sage wash on the ground), seen from the side, facing left, its beak pointing left and level, its long neck in a gentle S curve, its wings folded: a bird that has stopped at the end of a trail and stands looking at a sign just outside the picture on its left. Anatomically correct: one head, one long beak, exactly two thin legs.

Portrait format. The whole bird is inside the frame, centred, filling about three quarters of the frame's height, with clear empty paper margins on every side and nothing cut off. The grass tuft fades out softly into the paper on both sides, no hard edge. Plain empty warm cream paper, evenly lit, no vignette. No sign, no post, no water, no reeds, no mountains, no mist, no sun, no other birds. An American, California scene: no Chinese characters, no seals or stamps, no text, no signature, no watermark, no border. Nothing red anywhere. No pen outlines, no fine feather detail, no stippling, not a soft storybook illustration: a bold freehand ink painting.
