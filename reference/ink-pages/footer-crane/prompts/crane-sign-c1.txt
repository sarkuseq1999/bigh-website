Two reference paintings are attached. The FIRST is our red-crowned crane at rest: copy its brush hand exactly (the same painter, the same sumi ink, the same bold few strokes: one loaded, wet, jet-black neck stroke; a white body that is bare unpainted paper held by one calligraphic contour line that swells, thins and breaks, with only two or three broad pale grey wash strokes under the wing; heavy jet-black tail plumes ending in split dry-brush tips; each leg one quick thin dark stroke with a small knot at the joint; the small crown a tiny touch of gold leaf, never red). The SECOND is the California park sign the crane will stand beside on our website: copy its loose watercolour sage-green grass tufts and its plain warm cream paper exactly. Do NOT paint the sign, its posts or its poppy.

Paint exactly ONE crane standing on two thin legs in a low tuft of sage-green grass (the same loose grass blades as at the sign's posts, with the same faint pale sage wash on the ground), seen from the side, facing left, bending its long neck forward and down in one slow, graceful curve, its beak pointing down to the lower left, as if looking closely at a small wildflower growing just outside the picture on its left, at the height of the grass. Its wings folded, its body level. Curious and gentle. Anatomically correct: one head, one long beak, exactly two thin legs.

Portrait format, a little wider than tall is fine. The whole bird is inside the frame, centred, filling about two thirds of the frame's height, with clear empty paper margins on every side and nothing cut off; the beak stays inside the frame with paper to its left. The grass tuft fades out softly into the paper on both sides, no hard edge. No flower in the picture. Plain empty warm cream paper, evenly lit, no vignette. No sign, no post, no water, no reeds, no mountains, no mist, no sun, no other birds. An American, California scene: no Chinese characters, no seals or stamps, no text, no signature, no watermark, no border. Nothing red anywhere. No pen outlines, no fine feather detail, no stippling, not a soft storybook illustration: a bold freehand ink painting.
