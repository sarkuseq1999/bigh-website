Paint a NEW picture. ONE reference picture is attached.

It is the hand to paint in: the Big Sur painting at the end of our homepage, ink and wash. Copy its way of painting exactly: drawn first with a sumi ink brush, lines that swell, thin and break, a few loaded dark accents and dry-brush strokes, then light, transparent colour washed over the ink, the wash not filling the lines exactly, with bare paper left between.

WHAT TO PAINT: Two California quail (Callipepla californica, California's state bird) in a low tuft of sage-green grass, both facing left. The one at the back stands tall and still, head up, keeping watch, its black teardrop plume curling forward over its forehead; the one in front bends its head down toward the lower left, as if looking at a small wildflower just outside the picture on its left, at the height of the grass.

A QUICK PAINTING: the whole picture in about a dozen brush strokes, as if painted in a minute. Edges soft and dissolving into the paper, or broken by the dry brush; never a dashed or dotted contour line running around the body, never hatched edges. Big areas of bare paper inside the animal, left as the light. Smooth paper and clean transparent washes: no speckled, mottled, grainy or digital-filter texture anywhere.

PAINT EACH QUAIL with a few confident ink brush strokes: one broken line for the round back and breast, one wet black stroke for the face held by a strip of bare paper, one quick curling stroke for the plume, two or three dry-brush strokes for the wing and tail. Then a pale blue-grey wash over the breast and back and a pale warm brown wash on the flanks, laid loosely over the ink. The eye a single dot. No feather pattern, no scales, no closed outline traced all around. Muted colours only (ink, blue-grey, warm brown, sage); no bright colour.

THE GRASS: a few quick ink-and-sage blades and a faint pale sage wash on the ground under the birds, fading softly into the paper on both sides, in the same hand.

Landscape format, wider than tall. Both birds and their grass inside the frame, together filling about the middle half of the frame's width, with clear empty paper margins on every side and nothing cut off; the bending bird's beak stays inside the frame with paper to its left. Plain empty warm cream paper, evenly lit, no vignette. No flower, no sign, no post, no fence, no sea, no hills, no people, no other animals. An American, California scene: no Chinese characters, no seals or stamps, no text, no signature, no watermark, no border. Nothing red, no gold. No mottled or speckled texture and no digital watercolour-filter look anywhere.
