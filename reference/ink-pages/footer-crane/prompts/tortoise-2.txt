Two reference paintings are attached. The FIRST is the bird that stands in our website's footer today, at rest in a tuft of grass: copy its brush hand and its layout (the same painter, the same sumi ink, the same bold few strokes, the same low tuft of loose sage-green grass blades with a faint pale sage wash on the ground, the same plain warm cream paper and the same empty paper margins). The SECOND is the California park sign this animal will stand beside on our website: copy its loose watercolour grass and its warm cream paper. Do NOT paint the sign, its posts or its poppy. Do NOT paint the crane.

Paint ONE small Mojave desert tortoise (Gopherus agassizii, California's state reptile, which shares the Mojave's spring poppy fields; NOT a giant Galapagos tortoise: a small tortoise about a foot long, its shell a moderate dome, not tall) in place of the crane, walking slowly through that same low grass, seen from the side and a little from the front, facing left. Its head reaches forward and DOWN on its neck, its nose lowered toward the grass, sniffing a small wildflower growing just outside the picture on its left, at the height of the grass. One front foot lifted mid-step: unhurried, steady, a long life lived at its own pace. A high domed shell. Anatomically correct: one head, four sturdy legs, one shell.

STYLE: a bold freehand ink painting with a little pale colour laid over it, like the first reference. The shell a few broad loaded strokes of grey and black ink with a warm muted brown wash, its plates suggested by a few dry broken strokes, never drawn one by one; the head, neck and legs a few quick strokes of warm grey ink. Muted colours only, in the sign's quiet palette (ink, warm brown, ochre, sage); no bright colour. No fine detail, no scale pattern, no stippling, no pen outlines, not a soft storybook illustration, not cute, not a cartoon.

Landscape format, wider than tall. The tortoise and its grass inside the frame, together filling about the middle half of the frame's width, with clear empty paper margins on every side and nothing cut off; its head stays inside the frame with paper to its left. The grass fades out softly into the paper on both sides, no hard edge. No flower in the picture. Plain empty warm cream paper, evenly lit, no vignette. No sign, no post, no rocks, no cactus, no sand dunes, no mountains, no sun, no other animals. An American, California scene: no Chinese characters, no seals or stamps, no text, no signature, no watermark, no border. Nothing red, no gold.

SECOND TAKE: paint it LOOSER and bolder than a field-guide watercolour, closer to the first reference's hand: fewer, bigger, wetter strokes, the ink allowed to pool and bleed, edges broken by dry brush, large areas left as bare paper. No hatched feather or fur edges, no outline traced around the animal, no mottled speckled texture and no digital watercolour-filter look anywhere: clean wet washes on smooth paper.
