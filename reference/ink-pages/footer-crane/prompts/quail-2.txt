Two reference paintings are attached. The FIRST is the bird that stands in our website's footer today, at rest in a tuft of grass: copy its brush hand and its layout (the same painter, the same sumi ink, the same bold few strokes, the same low tuft of loose sage-green grass blades with a faint pale sage wash on the ground, the same plain warm cream paper, the same size in the frame and the same empty paper margins). The SECOND is the California park sign these birds will stand beside on our website: copy its loose watercolour grass and its warm cream paper. Do NOT paint the sign, its posts or its poppy. Do NOT paint the crane.

Paint TWO California quail (Callipepla californica, California's state bird) in place of the crane, in that same tuft of grass, seen from the side, both facing left:
- The first stands tall and still at the back, head up, keeping watch: the covey's lookout. Its plume, the short black teardrop topknot that curls forward over its forehead, stands clearly above its head.
- The second, a little in front and to the left, bends its head forward and down toward the lower left, as if looking closely at a small wildflower growing just outside the picture on its left, at the height of the grass.
Plump round bodies, small heads, short legs mostly hidden in the grass. Anatomically correct: each bird one head, one short beak, one plume.

STYLE: a bold freehand ink painting with a little pale colour laid over it, like the first reference. Each bird a few loaded wet strokes of grey and black ink: a soft blue-grey wash for the breast and back, a warm muted brown wash on the flanks, the black face of the male held by one white line, the plume one confident black stroke. Muted colours only, in the sign's quiet palette (ink, blue-grey, warm brown, sage); no bright colour. No fine feather detail, no scale pattern, no stippling, no pen outlines, not a soft storybook illustration, not cute, not a cartoon.

Landscape format, wider than tall. Both birds and their grass inside the frame, together filling about the middle half of the frame's width, with clear empty paper margins on every side and nothing cut off; the bending bird's beak stays inside the frame with paper to its left. The grass tuft fades out softly into the paper on both sides, no hard edge. No flower in the picture. Plain empty warm cream paper, evenly lit, no vignette. No sign, no post, no fence, no water, no mountains, no mist, no sun, no other animals. An American, California scene: no Chinese characters, no seals or stamps, no text, no signature, no watermark, no border. Nothing red, no gold.

SECOND TAKE: paint it LOOSER and bolder than a field-guide watercolour, closer to the first reference's hand: fewer, bigger, wetter strokes, the ink allowed to pool and bleed, edges broken by dry brush, large areas left as bare paper. No hatched feather or fur edges, no outline traced around the animal, no mottled speckled texture and no digital watercolour-filter look anywhere: clean wet washes on smooth paper.
