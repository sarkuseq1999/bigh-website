THE STORY BESIDE IT: "I'd heard of propolis, but I didn't know what made the green kind different. Reading about its source in Minas Gerais, Brazil, helped me understand what I was buying." (Green propolis is the resin bees gather from a Brazilian shrub with narrow leaves, Baccharis dracunculifolia, called alecrim-do-campo.)

The first attached picture is the painting this one sits beside on the website: the same back porch of a California family home, the same golden hills and valley oak beyond its rail, painted by the same hand. Keep its hand, its colours, its porch table, its rail and its hills; this is the same place, in the afternoon. The other attached pictures are only the reference for the brush hand.

THE SUBJECT: On the same weathered wooden porch table, in warm afternoon light: a small round wooden cutting board, and on it a broken piece of honeycomb, its six-sided cells a few loose strokes, a little dark green-brown resin glistening at its broken edge. Lying beside the board, a fresh sprig of the Brazilian shrub: one thin stem with many small, narrow, bright green leaves and a few tiny pale cream flower tufts at its tips. Next to them, a small white linen napkin, folded. That is all: few things, lots of bare table.
