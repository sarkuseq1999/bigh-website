An ink-wash painting on one plain sheet of warm, light rice paper with fine fibres, seen flat from straight on, evenly lit, no shadow, no frame, no table under the paper, no text, no signature, no red seal, no stamp. Paint it the way a master ink painter works: a few confident, loose brush strokes, wet ink bleeding softly into the paper, dry-brush texture where the brush runs out, simplified forms that suggest rather than describe, lots of empty paper. It must look like a painting, expressive and artistic, with the brushwork of the reference picture: never a realistic drawing, never a detailed illustration, never a pencil sketch, never a 3D render. Copy only the reference's brushwork, never its subject: no bird, no crane. Sumi ink only, from deep black to soft grey washes, plus real gold leaf only where stated. Only the things named below are painted. The subject is American and Californian: no Asian objects, no Chinese characters, no pagodas, no bamboo. The painting fades out softly into empty paper on every side, with wide empty margins. Use far fewer strokes than an illustrator would: broad wet washes and a handful of bold, quick calligraphic strokes, like a painter working in two minutes. No fine hatching, no cross-hatching, no thin outline around every shape, no texture on every plank or blade of grass, no small details. Objects are suggested by one or two strokes each and parts are left unfinished, dissolving into the paper.

A thick wooden plank door with iron straps, set deep in a rough, white-washed adobe wall of an old California mission-style building, with a curved arch over the doorway and a few pale clay roof tiles along the top of the wall painted in grey ink. The door stands half open inward. Warm light spills out of the doorway across the threshold and down the one stone step, painted in real gold leaf: this light is the only gold in the painting. A loose climbing vine hangs over one corner of the wall, and a single agave plant stands in a clay pot beside the step. The wall's edges dissolve into the paper. The doorway is in the middle of the sheet.
