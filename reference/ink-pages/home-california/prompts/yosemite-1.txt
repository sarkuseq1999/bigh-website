ATTACHED: our current opening painting. Copy ONLY its hand and colours (the soft wet watercolour, pale blue-grey far, sage green near, lots of bare paper and mist) and its layout (scene on the right, bare paper on the left). Do NOT copy its mountains or its pine trees: paint a completely different place, described below.

THE PLACE: Yosemite Valley in California's Sierra Nevada, early morning.
- On the RIGHT: El Capitan, a huge sheer wall of smooth pale-grey granite with a rounded top, rising straight up from the valley floor (a smooth monolith, not jagged).
- Farther away, at about 60-70% across, just below the sun's spot: the unmistakable silhouette of Half Dome, a rounded granite dome with one side sliced off flat and vertical, pale blue-grey in the distance.
- A thin ribbon waterfall (Bridalveil Fall) drops down a cliff at the far right.
- The valley floor at the lower right: dark green forest of tall straight conifers (sugar pines and incense cedars: tall narrow spires, never twisted pines), with low morning mist lying in the valley between the trees.
- Toward the left the valley fades into pale mist and bare paper.

STYLE (shared by every scene):
A loose watercolour-and-ink landscape painting on plain warm white paper, wide format (landscape, 3:2, 1536 x 1024). The hand of a quick, confident watercolour: wet soft edges, colour bleeding into the paper, a few dry-brush streaks, very few strokes and lots of bare paper. Soft, calm colours: pale blue-grey in the distance, sage green and muted straw-ochre nearer, dark ink only in a few small accents. The more it looks like a painting, the better. Not photo-real, not a detailed drawing, not a storybook illustration.

It is an American, California scene in the spirit of the California plein-air watercolour painters, NOT an Asian ink painting: no tall pointed misty peaks, no twisted pine trees clinging to rocks, no pagodas, no characters, no seals or stamps.

COMPOSITION (the picture is the background of a website's first screen):
- The top 36% of the picture is empty bare paper sky: no sun disc, no birds, no clouds. A gold sun and a flying bird will be added on top later. The sun will sit just above the horizon at about 69% across, so keep that spot clear.
- The horizon (the farthest layer of the scene) runs across the full width at about 38-44% down.
- The main subject fills the RIGHT 45% of the picture, from the horizon down to the bottom edge, nearest and darkest at the lower right.
- The LEFT half below the horizon stays almost bare paper: only very pale, faint washes that fade out, because big words will be set there.
- The bottom edge fades softly into bare paper; no hard edges, no frame.

Never: no sun, no birds, no people, no animals, no cars, no text, letters or numbers anywhere, no signature, no seal or stamp, no frame or border, no gold leaf.
