A short, knobbly piece of fresh turmeric root lying on its side on the left of the middle, its thin skin a few dry-brush strokes of warm brown-grey ink with the faint rings of the root, its freshly cut end facing us. In front of it, a little to the right and lower, lies one round slice cut from the root, flat on the paper, seen a little from above: a ragged ring of brown-grey skin around a cut face of bright, real gold leaf. From that slice, the gold has soaked out into the rice paper: a large, soft watercolour bloom of warm golden-ochre wash and gold leaf spreading outward through the paper's fibres around and below the slice, its edges feathered and irregular like wet watercolour bleeding into absorbent paper, a slightly darker golden rim where the wet edge dried, fading to pale gold and then to bare paper. The root's own cut end is gold leaf too. The bloom covers about half of the picture. Nothing else. Calm, warm, luminous.
