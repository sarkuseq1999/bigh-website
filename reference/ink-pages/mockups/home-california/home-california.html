1

Anna's hummingbird

8.5 / 10 My pick

California's year-round hummingbird. For its size it burns energy faster than almost any animal, and its flight muscles are packed with mitochondria, the "tiny power plants" in your cells.

Why it works

It ties straight to the headline: a tiny body running on its cells' energy. Lively, and plainly American.

What holds it back

Its wings are painted too big and feathery for a real hummingbird; a build would repaint them narrow and blurred. The pink throat is a second colour beside the gold.

2

Golden eagle

8 / 10

A golden eagle gliding over the Sierra. It can live 30 years, and its name matches the gold.

Why it works

The most American of the three, and its long glide suits the long stroke.

What holds it back

It reads as strength and power, a little like a bank's logo, more than calm health. The heaviest, most detailed painting on the screen.

3

Sandhill crane

7.5 / 10

The grey American crane from before, now with the sun fixed.

Why it works

The calmest, and the least work: the crane also flies in the homepage's last painting and stands in every footer.

What holds it back

A crane flying past a gold sun is still a classic Japanese and Chinese picture, even in grey.

D

Giant sequoias in the morning

9 / 10 Your pick

The morning sun shines into a grove of giant sequoias, trees that live more than 3,000 years. (Round 2: the sun here was squeezed left; fixed above.)

How it ties to the words

Long life: some of the oldest living things on earth. Energy starting, not ending: a morning sun, in the same gold as "cells."

What holds it back

It is painted with more detail than the rest of the site. The sun moves a little left, into the open light beside the trees. Sandhill cranes winter in the valley below, not in the groves.

A

Big Sur sunset

8.5 / 10

The sun sets over the Pacific and lays a gold path on the water. Highway 1 and its arch bridge run along the cliffs.

Why it works

Only the West Coast sees the sun set into the sea. Nobody reads it as China.

What holds it back

A sun low over the Pacific is always setting, which can read as "the end of the day" for a brand about aging. Sandhill cranes live inland, not on the coast.

B

Golden hills

8.5 / 10

"The Golden State": low summer hills, a big valley oak and a ranch fence under the sun.

Why it works

The softest and calmest of the three, and plainly California.

What holds it back

Both About test openings are golden hills too, so the two pages would look alike.

C

Yosemite

7.5 / 10

El Capitan, Half Dome under the sun, a waterfall and morning mist in the valley.

Why it works

Americans know these two rocks at a glance.

What holds it back

Misty cliffs and pine trees are the closest to today's look. On a wide screen the sun floats in empty sky.
