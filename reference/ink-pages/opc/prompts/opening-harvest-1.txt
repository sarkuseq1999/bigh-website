STYLE: An ink-and-wash painting on plain white paper, in the spirit of the California plein-air watercolour painters. The land is first drawn and shaded with a brush in grey and black ink, quickly and loosely, with dry-brush strokes; then a few light, transparent watercolour washes are laid over it. The sky is one big wet wash with no ink in it. The ink does the work on the land; the colour is light and calm. Lots of bare paper. You can see every brush stroke; soft bleeding edges where the paper was wet, crisp ones where it was dry. Richness comes from brushwork and ink, never from fine or photographic detail. The more it looks like a painting by a grown-up painter, the better. Not photo-real, no smooth digital gradients, no pen outlines, not a storybook or greeting-card illustration, not a cartoon. An American, California scene, NOT an Asian ink painting: no misty peaks, no bands of fog, no pine tree clinging to a rock, no cranes, no boats.

THE PICTURE: the opening painting of a product page: a clear late-summer morning in a California vineyard just before harvest, the hills behind it dry and golden, the vines heavy with ripe dark grapes, and almost all of it is SKY. Large dark lettering and a product will be laid over it later, so the painting must stay simple, calm and light. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

COMPOSITION, top to bottom (keep these heights):
- From the top edge down to 60% of the height: open sky. One big wet wash of clear cerulean blue, the blue of a cloudless California late-summer morning: mid-pale at the top, much paler and a touch warmer toward the hills, uneven as a real wash is, with granulation, brush direction showing, and one or two soft backruns. Two small soft clouds left as bare white paper, high up near the top corners only. No sun, no birds, no hard-edged clouds.
- From 60% to 68%: far, low rolling hills across the whole width, dry and golden: light washes of gold-ochre and straw with a little soft blue in the farthest ridge, their top a gentle, low line that never rises above 60% of the height. Three or four dark round valley oaks drawn small in grey ink on the slopes, and a few faint lines of far vineyard rows. Calm and pale.
- From 68% to 75%: the nearest vineyard, in the foreground. From the left edge to 40% across and from 60% across to the right edge: the ends of a few rows of grapevines in full deep-green leaf on short weathered posts, drawn quickly in grey ink with green washes over them; under the leaves hang a few bunches of ripe dark purple-blue grapes, each bunch painted as ONE loose dark violet shape with a few round touches at its edge, never as a field of separate dots. In the very middle of the picture (from 42% to 58% across) the ground is flat, low and bare, only short dry grass: an EMPTY spot where an object will be placed later. Nothing stands there, no vine, no post.
- From 75% to 80%: the dry grass thins out downward into a few loose dry-brush strokes and specks.
- The bottom 20% of the picture: completely bare white paper. Nothing there.

EDGES: the sky, the hills and the vineyard run off the left and right edges, and the sky off the top edge, with no fade, no vignette and no border. The bottom ends in bare white paper, never in a straight painted line.

NEVER: no text, letters or numbers anywhere, no signature, no seal, no frame or border, no gold paint, no bottles, jars, glasses, barrels, crates or products, no pills or capsules, no people, no animals, no buildings, no barn, no winery, no fence beyond the vines' own short posts, no road, no red or rust clustered dots, no glow, no halo.
