STYLE: An ink-and-watercolour painting on plain warm white paper, in the spirit of the California plein-air watercolour painters. The subject is first drawn and shaded with a brush in grey and black ink, quickly and loosely, with dry-brush strokes; then a few light, transparent watercolour washes are laid over it. Lots of bare paper. You can see every brush stroke; soft bleeding edges where the paper was wet, crisp ones where it was dry. Richness comes from brushwork and ink, never from fine or photographic detail. The more it looks like a painting by a grown-up painter, the better. Not photo-real, no smooth digital gradients, no pen outlines, not a botanical-textbook plate, not a storybook or greeting-card illustration, not a cartoon. NOT an Asian ink painting: no rice paper, no calligraphy, no red seal.

THE PICTURE: one chapter painting of a product page about OPCs, the antioxidant compounds that come from grape SEEDS. Portrait, 1024 x 1536. Seen up close. The cut grape is the main subject and is LARGE.

- Top half (from about 5% to 48% of the height): a small cluster of six or seven ripe California red-wine grapes still on their short green-brown stem, with one broad grape leaf behind them to one side and a curling tendril. Each grape is about a sixth of the picture's width: deep violet-purple to blue-black with the pale dusty bloom real grapes have, painted as loose wet dabs, a little lighter where the light falls from the upper left. Never shiny, never a field of identical dots. The leaf: sage and olive green washes over loose grey ink.
- Bottom half (from about 52% to 90% of the height), lying on the bare paper in front: ONE grape cut in half, the two halves side by side, cut faces up, slightly tilted toward us, together spanning about 85% of the picture's width (each half about 40% of the width). Their flesh is pale, translucent green-violet with a few loose brush marks for its fibres, the dark violet skin a thin rim round each half. In each half sit two or three pear-shaped grape seeds, each about a tenth of the picture's width, laid in REAL GOLD LEAF: flat, metallic, slightly crinkled and broken at the edges like gilding pressed onto paper. The seeds are the only gold in the picture.
Generous bare paper on every side. The bottom 7% of the picture is bare paper.

NEVER: no text, letters or numbers, no signature, no seal, no frame or border, no glow, no halo, no light shining through the fruit, no sparkle, no haze or wash of colour around anything, no gold anywhere but the seeds, no knife, no cutting board, no plate, no table, no background, no sky, no bottles, glasses or capsules, no people, no animals, no red or rust clustered dots.
