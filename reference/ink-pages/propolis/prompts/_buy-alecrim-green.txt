A low, wide bed of alecrim, the Brazilian shrub that bees gather green propolis resin from in Minas Gerais, growing across the lower part of the picture as a soft bed for an object to stand in its middle: slender, upright stems in loose black ink and many small, narrow, needle-like leaves painted in a soft, muted sage-green watercolour wash (the only colour besides ink and gold). At the very tips of seven or eight shoots, a tiny glistening bead of resin in real gold leaf. The bed is about 85% of the picture's width and about a third of its height; its middle third is a little lower and sparser; its two ends feather out into the paper. Nothing else: no bee, no sky, no hills, no ground line.
