A section of an old, upright tree trunk where wild honeybees live, filling the picture from top to bottom, its bark painted in a few bold dry-brush strokes of black and grey ink. In the middle of the trunk, a dark oval hollow: the entrance of the bees' nest. Around the hollow, three or four long, thin, branching cracks run across the bark, and every crack is filled with a fine, continuous, unbroken line of real gold leaf, exactly like Japanese kintsugi, where broken pottery is mended with gold: the gold lines are clearly the cracks sealed shut, bright and crisp against the ink. Four small honeybees, each only a few quick strokes (a dark striped body, pale grey wash wings), one at the rim of the hollow and three in the air near it. Calm, quiet, precious.
