A hand-painted map of Brazil, the whole country's outline recognisable at a glance, painted loosely in soft grey ink wash with a confident, slightly broken dark ink edge, like an explorer's watercolour chart, filling the upper-left two-thirds of the picture. Inside it, the state of Minas Gerais (inland in the south-east of Brazil, north of Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo) is painted in real gold leaf, glowing, with its own thin ink edge. One tiny honeybee, only a few quick strokes, flies just beside Minas Gerais. No other states drawn, no text, no letters, no labels, no compass, no grid, no rivers, no other countries. The lower right third of the picture is open, empty paper. Nothing else.
