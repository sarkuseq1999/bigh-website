One classic American wooden beehive standing on the ground, the kind kept in California orchards: two stacked rectangular wooden boxes with a flat lid on top, seen from the front and a little from above so the flat top of the lid shows, painted in loose, confident ink lines and pale grey washes, the wood grain hinted with dry brush. A narrow entrance slot at the bottom, with four or five small honeybees around it, each only a few quick strokes, one in flight. A few wild grasses at its foot. The top of the lid is flat, level and empty. The hive is about half the picture's width and fills its lower 60%, with empty paper above it. Nothing else. No gold.
