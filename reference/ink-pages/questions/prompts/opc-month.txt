The attached picture (a flying crane) is the style to copy exactly: loose sumi-e brushwork on warm plain paper (#F6F3EC), a few confident strokes, wide pale grey washes that bleed softly into the paper, rich black only in a few accents laid in one stroke, dry-brush edges with flying white, and a great deal of bare paper; nothing is drawn, outlined or detailed. Do not paint the crane; paint only this subject in its hand: Two months of a daily habit: two hand-ruled calendar pages pinned side by side on a wall, each by one tack. Each page is a loosely brushed grid of exactly thirty small squares: six squares across and five rows down (6 x 5 = 30 on each page, 60 in all). Every square has one quick single brush tick; the last tick on the second page is fresh and darker than the rest. Nothing is written in the squares or around them: no numbers, no month names, no days of the week. Grey and black ink only, except a thin transparent watercolour tint where one is named. Generous empty paper on every side; the edges dissolve into the paper. Calm and quiet. It must look painted with a brush, never drawn: no pen outlines, no hard edges, no fine detail, no photo look. Landscape format, 4:3. No text, letters, numbers, signs or lettering anywhere; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame; no Asian cultural motifs (no chopsticks, no rice bowls, no tea ceremony). No lab glassware. Nothing red; no red, orange or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
