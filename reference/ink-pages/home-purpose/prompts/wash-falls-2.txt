STYLE: A loose, bold watercolour painted quickly on location with big wet brushes, in the spirit of the California Style watercolour painters of the 1930s. You can see how every part of it was painted.
- Big simple shapes. Each headland is one or two wet washes, its far side lost into the next one. No single rocks, bushes, ripples or blades of grass, except a few near ones.
- Colours bleed into each other wet-in-wet. Where a wash dried there are soft blooms and crisp dried edges; pigment granulates in the shadows.
- Lots of bare white paper left as the light: the sky, the surf, the sun's glitter on the sea, the edges of the cliffs catching the sun.
- The sea is a few long, broken horizontal strokes of blue-grey with gaps of bare paper for the glitter, not painted waves.
- The foreground is quick dry-brush flicks for the grass, a few loose dots and dabs of orange for the poppies, the trail one stroke of bare paper.
- A few confident dark strokes (deep blue-violet and umber, almost ink) in the nearest cliff shadows, for depth.
- Far headlands: each one a single flat pale wash, paler and bluer the farther it is.
- The bottom of the painting ends in ragged, broken brush edges on bare paper.
- Calm colours, like the rest of the website's paintings: muted sage green, pale straw and dry-grass ochre on the land, soft blue-grey and violet-grey in the shadows, a blue-grey sea. No bright orange and no saturated gold on the hills: the only strong gold will be the gold-leaf sun added later. The evening light is a pale warm wash.
- Smooth, transparent wet washes with soft blooms and granulation; no mottled, speckled or noisy texture, no digital filter look.
Not photo-real, no fine detail, no smooth digital gradients, no sharp texture, no pen outlines, not a storybook illustration.

It is an American, California scene, NOT an Asian ink painting: no pine or cypress trees, no pointed misty peaks, no sea of mist or bands of fog, no pagodas, no cranes, no characters, no seals or stamps.

THE PEOPLE: an older couple and their dog standing on the cliff-top trail looking out, small and far (the people about 5% of the picture's height), each just two or three touches of the brush, no faces, no details.

COMPOSITION (the wide painting that closes a website's homepage; only a wide band of it is shown), as in the first picture:
- The top 45% of the picture is open evening sky: bare paper with at most a very pale warm wash near the horizon. No clouds, no birds, no sun disc. Big words will be set there.
- The sea's horizon runs level at about 59% down.
- A gold-leaf sun will be added later, sitting on the horizon at about 82% across, at the head of the glitter path. Keep that spot clear, with only a pale warm glow around it.
- Land and sea run off both the left and the right edge, with no fade at the sides; the land may rise to 42% down at the far left, never higher.
- Below 92% down everything ends in bare paper; no hard edge, no frame.

THE SCENE: McWay Falls in Big Sur at evening. From the cliff-top trail on the left we look down into a small sheltered cove: a slender waterfall drops from the cliff onto a pale sand beach, painted as one stroke of bare paper down the cliff's shadow with a little splash of paper at its foot. The cove is one wet wash of soft turquoise bleeding into blue-grey, its surf a broken line of bare paper. The couple and their dog stand on the trail at about 28% across and 64% down. A few big rocks off the cove, each one or two strokes. Beyond the cove, two or three headlands recede down the coast, each paler and bluer. The open Pacific fills the right, glittering under the sun's spot.

NEVER: no sun disc, no text, letters or numbers, no signature, no seal, no frame or border, no gold, no birds, no cars, no buildings, no lab glassware, no red or rust clustered dots, no glow, no halo.
