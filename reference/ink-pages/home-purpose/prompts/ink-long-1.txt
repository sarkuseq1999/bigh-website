STYLE: An ink-and-wash painting on warm white paper. The whole scene is first drawn and shaded with a brush in grey and black ink, quickly and loosely; then a few light, transparent watercolour washes are laid over it: dry-grass gold and sage on the bluffs, blue-grey on the sea and the far headlands, a few dabs of poppy orange, a pale warm glow by the sun. The ink does the work; the colour is light and calm.
- The nearest bluff is the darkest: bold, confident strokes and dry-brush rocks in deep ink.
- Each headland behind is paler and softer, the farthest one a single pale grey-blue wash.
- Lots of bare paper: the sky, the surf, the sun's glitter on the sea, the sunlit edges of the cliffs.
- The sea is a few long broken horizontal strokes, with gaps of bare paper for the glitter.
- You can see every brush stroke; soft bleeding edges where the paper was wet, crisp ones where it was dry.
Not photo-real, no fine detail everywhere, no smooth digital gradients, no pen outlines, not a storybook illustration.
