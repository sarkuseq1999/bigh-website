A loose sumi-e ink-wash landscape painted on plain warm white paper, wide format (landscape, 3:2), in grey and black ink, with warm gold only in the flowers and nowhere else. The hand of a quick, confident ink-and-mist painting: wet soft edges, ink bleeding into the paper, dry-brush streaks, very few strokes and lots of bare paper. The more it looks like a painting, the better.

The place is California in spring: rolling foothills in a wildflower superbloom, the hills covered in California poppies.

Composition:
- The upper 55% of the picture is empty paper sky; words will be set there later. No sun, no birds, no clouds: bare paper, with at most a very faint pale grey mist just above the farthest ridge.
- The lower 45%: three or four layers of soft rolling hills running the full width of the picture. The farthest ridge pale grey and half lost in mist; the nearer hills darker grey ink, their slopes laid with broad wet strokes and a little dry-brush grass.
- The poppies: wide drifts of California poppies sweep across the nearer hills from the left edge to the right edge, the centre included. Each poppy is one quick little cup-shaped brush dab of two or three petals in warm golden ochre, like gold leaf. They are larger and fuller along the bottom edge (a few there with loose grey-green stems and lacy, feathery leaves), smaller further up the slopes, and on the far slopes they melt into a soft gold haze. They must read as painted flowers, never as dots, spots or glitter.
- Three or four small dark ink dabs for valley oaks on one far ridge.

The gold is a warm golden ochre like gold leaf: never orange-red, never red, no glow, no sparkle. No people, no animals, no buildings, no fences, no roads, no sun, no text, letters, numbers or signs anywhere, no signature, seal or stamp, no frame or border. Not photo-real, not a detailed botanical drawing, not a soft storybook illustration. An American, California scene: no Asian elements, no pagodas, no characters, no pine trees on rocks.
