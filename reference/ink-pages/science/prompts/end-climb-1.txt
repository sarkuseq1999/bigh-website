THE IDEA: the climber. A clear, bright summer morning in Yosemite Valley, California, no mist at all. High on a sunlit wall of warm, pale golden-grey granite, a fit woman in her late fifties climbs, reaching up with one hand for the next hold, the other hand gripping the rock, one foot pressed on a small ledge. She wears a pale sun shirt, dark climbing trousers, a helmet and a harness; a rope runs down from her harness out of the picture's bottom edge, a few small metal pieces on her harness. She is focused and strong, not afraid. The wall rises on the right of the picture, its great clean cracks and corners in a few sure ink strokes. Far below and beyond, the floor of Yosemite Valley: a green meadow, a river glinting, a carpet of small pines; across the valley Half Dome stands in clear warm light, small and pale. Lots of air and space under her.

SIZE AND PLACE: the wall fills the right of the picture from about 58% across to the right edge, top to bottom. The climber is about 34% of the picture's height, on the wall's left edge at about 62% to 74% across, 30% to 64% down. Her reaching hand, high on the rock, is plain to see against the pale granite. Half Dome is small, at about 25% across, its top at about 52% down. The valley floor lies along the bottom quarter, far below.

COLOUR: warm granite (pale gold-grey, sunlit), clear soft blue air, sage-green meadow and pines far below; her shirt a soft coral, the one warm note. Clear and sunny, no fog, no cloud bands.

Paint a NEW picture; the attached painting gives only the way of painting. Loose, quick ink brush strokes, light washes, lots of bare paper. Not a Chinese mountain painting: no misty peaks, no pine clinging to a rock, no waterfall.
