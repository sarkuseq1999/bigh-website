TWO PAINTINGS ARE ATTACHED. The first (a grandfather and a small child walking hand in hand on a trail through golden California hills) shows the two people to paint: the same grandfather and the same child, in the same clothes and colours. He: a pale straw sun hat, a pale shirt with the sleeves rolled to the elbow, khaki trousers, brown walking shoes, a fit man of about seventy. The child, about five: a little pale sun hat, a soft blue t-shirt, tan shorts. Take nothing else from it: this is a new scene and a new moment. The second painting (the Big Sur coast) gives only the way of painting.

THE PICTURE: the last view of a slow zoom on a website's Science page. The zoom starts inside one living cell, pulls back to the grandfather's hand holding the child's small hand, then to this picture, where the words "Why start with the cell? Everything you do, from a clear thought to a long walk, runs on energy your cells make." stand in the sky. The picture it replaces (the same two walking away from us down a trail) was calm but boring: nothing happened in it. This one must have a moment in it, something happening, alive at first glance.
