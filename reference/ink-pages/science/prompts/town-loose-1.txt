THE PICTURE: the first attached picture shows the place and the layout of a painting for a website's Science page: San Francisco's Victorian houses climbing a hill at early dusk, the bay and the East Bay hills beyond. Paint it again as a NEW picture, landscape 1536 x 1024, with the same layout (the same rows of houses in the same places, the same hill, the same bay at the right, the same cypress trees), but painted far more loosely, the way the second attached painting (the Big Sur coast) is painted: drawn quickly with a brush in grey and black ink, then a few light transparent washes (cream, pale blue-grey, sage, a soft butter yellow on some houses). Far fewer details: the far houses are only a few touches of the brush each, the near ones a few confident strokes for their gables and bay windows, no fine trim, no railings. Soft bleeding edges, lots of bare paper. It should look like a painting, not an illustration.

THE WINDOWS: keep many windows, about 80 to 120, as small separate dabs and rectangles of bare white paper (each still clearly a window shape, the near ones bigger), spread over the whole town. Every window is UNLIT: bare white paper inside, no yellow, no light anywhere. The lights will be added later.

COMPOSITION: as the first picture: the town from about 22% to 88% down across the full width, running off the left and right edges; open pale dusk sky above (bare paper, a very pale peach wash near the hills); bare paper below the nearest houses, with no hard edge. No people, no cars, no signs, no sun, no moon.
