TWO PAINTINGS ARE ATTACHED. The first (a grandfather and a small child walking hand in hand on a trail through golden California hills) is the scene this picture belongs to: paint the same two people, in the same clothes and colours, in the same light. The second (the Big Sur coast) gives only the way of painting.

THE PICTURE: a view on the way of a slow zoom on a website's Science page. The zoom starts inside one living cell, pulls back to this picture, then pulls back again to the first attached painting. So this is that painting seen very close: only the two joined hands. It fills the whole window with no words on it. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: seen from behind, close: the grandfather's hand holding the child's small hand, as in the first painting. His bare, tanned forearm comes straight down from the top edge, a little left of the middle, the rolled cuff of his pale shirt just showing at the top edge. His hand is large and relaxed, its back toward us, the fingers folded round the child's hand. The child's small forearm comes in from the right edge, a little below the middle, almost level, the hem of a soft blue short sleeve just showing at the right edge; only the child's wrist and the heel of the small hand show inside the big one. Behind them, out of focus: at the left, his warm grey-khaki trouser leg in two or three broad strokes; beyond, the golden grass and the pale trail as soft wet washes, and a hint of the far blue-grey range near the top right.

HOW THE HANDS ARE PAINTED: loosely, in a few sure strokes each. One broken ink brush line along the shaded side of the forearm and the hand; a warm skin wash left to pool at the wrist and the knuckles; bare paper for the light along the back of his hand. The back of his hand is one broad, calm patch of warm skin wash, the largest plain shape in the picture. NO fingernails drawn, NO knuckle creases, NO veins, NO wrinkles, no age spots: the fingers end in simple soft strokes. It reads at once as a big hand holding a small one, and it is plainly a painting.

SIZE AND PLACE (important): the joined hands are at the middle of the picture (about 50% across, 55% down). His hand, from wrist to fingertips, is about 36% of the picture's height. Both hands together fit inside the middle quarter of the picture's width.

COMPOSITION:
- Only the two forearms, the two hands, the edge of each sleeve and a little of his trouser leg. No faces, no torsos, no hats.
- The washes run off all four edges with no fade and no vignette. A little bare paper in the upper left is fine.
- No ring, no watch, no bracelet, no toy, nothing held in the hands.
