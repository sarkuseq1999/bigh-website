THE PICTURE: the second view of a slow zoom on a website's Science page. The first view was a walker on a trail in golden California hills; this one has moved in close on the walker's hand; the next will be one cell of that hand. A first take drew the hand too carefully (every knuckle crease and fingernail), like a realistic illustration. This take must be a loose painting. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: seen close, from the side: an older walker's hand and forearm, pale blue-grey sleeve rolled to the elbow, hanging relaxed at their side in mid-stride, the fingertips just brushing the tops of tall dry golden grass. One or two California poppies low in the grass at the left. Behind, the golden hill and far blue-grey range as two soft wet washes. The hand is large: about 45% of the picture's height, a little right of the middle.

HOW THE HAND IS PAINTED: in six or eight sure strokes. One broken ink brush line for its outer contour, a single warm skin wash left to pool at the wrist and knuckles, bare paper for the light along the back of the hand. Five fingers, the right shape and calm, but NO fingernails drawn, NO knuckle creases, NO veins, NO wrinkles: the fingers end in simple soft strokes. It reads as a hand at once and is plainly a painting.

COMPOSITION:
- Only the hand, the forearm, the rolled sleeve and a little of the hip and trouser leg behind it (two or three broad sage-grey strokes). No face, no torso.
- Open bare paper in the upper left third.
- The grass runs off the left, right and bottom edges with no fade and no vignette.

Paint a NEW picture; the attached painting gives only the way of painting. Loose, wet, quick; lots of bare paper.
