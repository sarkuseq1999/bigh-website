THE PICTURE: a painting for a website's Science page, beside the words "You have about 37 trillion cells, and each one makes its own energy." Later, the sunflowers in it will turn gold a few at a time, as the first sun reaches them: every flower turns its own face to the light and makes its own energy from it, as each cell makes its own. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: a field of sunflowers in California's Sacramento Valley (Yolo County, near Woodland and Davis) at sunrise in early summer. Rows of tall sunflowers run away from us toward a line of round valley oaks and a small farmhouse and barn far off; behind them the low, soft blue-grey line of the Coast Range hills.

THE FLOWERS: about 80 to 140 flower heads, clearly separated, all facing us: each is a round dark ink centre in a ring of loose brushed petals; the nearest ones big, near the bottom, the far ones small. Every flower is still in the shade before sunrise: its petals are bare white paper or the palest cream inside their ink strokes. No yellow, no gold, no orange anywhere yet. The leaves and stalks in loose sage and deep sage washes.

COMPOSITION:
- The field fills the picture from about 35% to 90% down, across the full width, running off the left and right edges with no fade at the sides.
- Above it: a calm dawn sky, bare paper with only a very pale peach wash low over the hills. No sun disc.
- Below about 90% down: bare paper; the nearest stalks fade out softly; no hard edge.
- No people, no tractors, no fences, no signs, no birds, no windmill.
