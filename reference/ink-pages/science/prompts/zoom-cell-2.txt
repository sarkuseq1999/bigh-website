THE PICTURE: the last view of a slow zoom on a website's Science page, beside the words "Why start with the cell? Everything you do runs on energy your cells make." The earlier views were a walker on golden hills, then the walker's hand; this is ONE living cell of that hand. A first take came out like a tidy schoolbook diagram with fat bean shapes that looked like peanuts. This take must be a real painting: wet, loose, quiet, a little mysterious, the kind of small watercolour study a plein-air painter would make. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: one single large cell, alone on bare paper, a little right of centre.
- NO drawn outline round it. The cell is a big, soft, irregular rounded pool of very pale wet wash (blue-grey running into sage and a little warm grey) whose edge is simply where the wet wash stopped: crisp and darker in some places where the pigment settled, lost into the paper in others, with one or two blooms and back-runs. Broken dry-brush ink marks along part of the lower edge only.
- The nucleus: one deep blue-grey ink-and-wash blot, off centre, wet-in-wet, darker on one side, its edge bleeding into the cell.
- The mitochondria: about twenty-five SLENDER gold marks scattered through the whole cell, thicker round the nucleus and thinning toward the edge. Each is one single short brush stroke of flat, matte, warm gold, like a sliver of gold leaf laid on the paper: long and thin (about five times longer than wide), gently curved, of different lengths, pointing every which way, a few lying across each other or forking. No ink outline round them, no lines inside them. They are the only strong colour and the first thing the eye finds.
- A few pale grey curved ink strokes near the nucleus, barely there.

THE GOLD: for this order only, the rule "no gold paint" is lifted for these marks and nowhere else. Flat and matte, like gold leaf: no glow, no shine, no sparkle, no halo, no yellow light around them.

NEVER: no bean, peanut, sausage or capsule shapes; no neat even spacing; no outlines round the gold; nothing that could read as germs or worms: no heads, tails, hairs, spikes or segments, no small dots in clusters, no red, pink, purple or bright green. No second cell. No text, labels, pointers, scale bar, microscope, lens or ring.

COMPOSITION:
- The cell is about 78% of the picture's height. Left of it, about a quarter of the picture is bare warm paper.
- It should look like a painting someone would frame.
