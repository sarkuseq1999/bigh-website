TWO PAINTINGS ARE ATTACHED. The first (an older man rowing a racing single scull on a still California lake at sunrise, golden hills with oaks behind, ochre ripple rings round his oar) is the picture to paint again: the same rower, the same clothes and colours, the same place, light and loose hand. The second (the Big Sur coast) gives only the way of painting.

THE PICTURE: the first painting again as a TALL picture for a phone's screen, where the words "Why start with the cell? Everything you do runs on energy your cells make." stand in its sky. Portrait, 1024 x 1536.

SIZE AND PLACE (important):
- The top 44% of the picture is open sky: bare paper with only the palest warm wash, nothing in it, so that large dark words can sit there.
- The far shore with golden hills and oaks along about 50% to 58% down; the lake below.
- The rower, seen from the side, is about 22% of the picture's height, at about 30% to 70% across, his cap at about 58% down, the hull at about 74% down. The near oar reaches the water at about 34% across, 80% down. Nothing that matters is in the outer 15% at the left or the right.

THE GOLD (important): paint ONLY the ripple rings round the oar blade and a few fainter ones on the water as thin strokes of flat, even, pale warm ochre-yellow (they will be laid in gold leaf later). No other yellow on the water.

Paint a NEW picture. Loose, quick ink brush strokes, light washes, lots of bare paper. A modern racing shell with long oars on an American lake; no mist.
