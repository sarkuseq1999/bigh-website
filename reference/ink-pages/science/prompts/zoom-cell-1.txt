THE PICTURE: the last view of a slow zoom on a website's Science page, beside the words "Why start with the cell? Everything you do runs on energy your cells make." The earlier views were a walker on golden hills, then the walker's hand; this is ONE cell of that hand, painted the way a naturalist would paint a specimen in a field notebook, in ink and wash. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: one single large human cell, seen in cross-section, in the middle of bare paper. Its outline is one loose, slightly irregular rounded shape drawn with a wet ink brush line that thickens and thins and breaks in places (not a perfect circle, not a pen line). Inside:
- a pale, almost bare-paper interior with a faint blue-grey and sage wash pooling here and there;
- the nucleus: one soft round blue-grey wash a little off centre, darker at one side, with a wet bleeding edge;
- a few pale curved ink strokes near the nucleus (folded membranes), barely there;
- NINE mitochondria spread evenly through the cell, well apart from each other: each a plump bean or short sausage shape about one eighth of the cell's width, drawn with an ink outline and three to five short curved ink strokes inside (its folds), and filled with a flat, matte, warm gold-ochre wash, like a small piece of gold leaf. They point in different directions. These nine are the only strong colour in the picture and the first thing the eye finds.

THE GOLD: for this order only, the rule "no gold paint" is lifted for the nine mitochondria and nowhere else. The gold is flat and matte, like gold leaf on paper: no glow, no shine, no sparkle, no rays, no halo, no yellow light around them.

NEVER: nothing that could read as germs or bacteria: no small dots in clusters, no hairs or tails on the cell, no spikes, no fuzzy edges all round, no red, pink, purple or green. No second cell. No text, labels, pointers, scale bar, microscope, lens or ring.

COMPOSITION:
- The cell is about 80% of the picture's height, centred. Around it, bare warm paper with at most a faint wash under it.
- Calm and beautiful first: it should look like a painting someone would frame, not a textbook diagram.
