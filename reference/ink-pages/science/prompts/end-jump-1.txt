THE IDEA: Grandma's cannonball. A bright summer afternoon at Lake Tahoe, California: crystal-clear water, turquoise near the shore over pale sand and round granite boulders, deep blue farther out, a line of Jeffrey pines on the far shore and the Sierra's rounded granite ridges beyond, under a clear sky. An old weathered wooden dock runs in from the bottom right. A fit grandmother in her late sixties, in a plain navy swimsuit and a white swim cap, has just leapt off the end of the dock: she hangs in mid-air above the water in a cannonball, knees hugged to her chest, laughing. On the dock behind her two grandchildren of about seven and nine, in bright swimsuits, jump up and down and cheer with their arms in the air; a striped towel lies on the boards. Under her the water is still smooth, waiting. It reads at once as joy, nerve and energy at any age.

SIZE AND PLACE: the grandmother, curled up in the air, is about 20% of the picture's height, at about 68% across and 46% to 66% down. Her hands, clasped round her shins, are plain to see. The dock comes in from the bottom right edge to about 72% across. The grandchildren stand on it at about 80% to 92% across. The far shore and the ridges lie along about 48% down. The lake fills the bottom half.

COLOUR: more colour than a plain landscape, but light and transparent: turquoise and blue-green water, warm grey granite, sage pines, the dock's silver-brown wood, the children's swimsuits two small clear notes (coral and yellow).

Paint a NEW picture; the attached painting gives only the way of painting. Loose, quick ink brush strokes, light washes, lots of bare paper.
