THE PICTURE: the last view of a slow zoom on a website's Science page, beside the words "You have about 37 trillion cells, and each one makes its own energy." The earlier views were a walker on golden hills, then the walker's hand; this is a close view of a few cells of that hand, side by side, painted the way a naturalist would paint a specimen in a field notebook, in ink and wash. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: about seven large cells fitted loosely together like the stones of an old dry-stone wall or a flagstone path: each cell an irregular rounded many-sided shape, a narrow gap of bare paper between neighbours. One cell in the middle is whole and the largest (about 45% of the picture's height); the others around it run off the edges of the picture. Each cell's outline is a loose wet ink brush line that thickens, thins and breaks. Inside each cell: a pale wash (blue-grey or sage, very light), one soft round blue-grey nucleus, and four to six mitochondria: plump bean shapes, well apart, each with an ink outline and a few short curved ink strokes inside, filled with a flat, matte, warm gold-ochre wash, like a small piece of gold leaf. The gold beans are the only strong colour and the first thing the eye finds.

THE GOLD: for this order only, the rule "no gold paint" is lifted for the mitochondria and nowhere else. Flat and matte, like gold leaf on paper: no glow, no shine, no sparkle, no halo.

NEVER: nothing that could read as germs or bacteria: no small dots in clusters, no hairs, tails or spikes, no fuzzy edges, no red, pink, purple or bright green. No text, labels, pointers, scale bar, microscope, lens or ring.

COMPOSITION:
- The cells fill the picture and run off all four edges, fading a little to bare paper toward the corners.
- Calm and beautiful first: a painting someone would frame, not a textbook diagram.
