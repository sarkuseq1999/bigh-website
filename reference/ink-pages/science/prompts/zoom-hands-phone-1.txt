TWO PAINTINGS ARE ATTACHED. The first (a close view, from behind, of a grandfather's hand holding a small child's hand, golden grass behind) is the picture to paint again: the same two hands, the same skin and sleeve colours, the same loose, wet way of painting. The second (the Big Sur coast) gives only the way of painting.

THE PICTURE: the first painting again as a TALL picture for a phone's screen. It is a view on the way of a slow zoom on a website's Science page (from inside one living cell, out to this hand, out to the grandfather and the child walking on golden hills). It fills the whole screen with no words on it. Portrait, 1024 x 1536.

THE SCENE: seen from behind, close. The grandfather's bare, tanned forearm comes straight down from the top edge at the middle of the picture, the rolled cuff of his pale shirt showing at the top edge. His hand is large and relaxed, its back toward us, the fingers folded round the child's small hand. The child's small forearm comes in from the right, almost level; it is short, and the hem of the child's soft blue short sleeve shows clearly at about 80% across (well inside the picture, not at its edge). Only the child's wrist and the heel of the small hand show inside the big one. Behind them, out of focus: at the left, his warm grey-khaki trouser leg in two or three broad strokes; below and beyond, the golden grass and the pale dirt trail as soft wet washes; a hint of the far blue-grey range and one or two orange poppies near the upper right.

HOW THE HANDS ARE PAINTED: as in the first painting. A warm skin wash left to pool at the wrist and the knuckles, bare paper for the light along the back of his hand, a broken ink brush line along the shaded side. The back of his hand is one broad, calm patch of warm skin wash. NO fingernails drawn, NO knuckle creases, NO veins, NO wrinkles, no age spots.

SIZE AND PLACE (important):
- The joined hands are at about 48% across and 58% down.
- His hand, from wrist to fingertips, is about 27% of the picture's height and about a third of the picture's width.
- Everything that matters (his forearm and hand, the child's wrist, forearm and blue sleeve) lies between 18% and 84% across: a phone shows only the middle of the picture's width.

COMPOSITION:
- Only the two forearms, the two hands, the edge of each sleeve and a little of his trouser leg. No faces, no torsos, no hats.
- The washes run off all four edges with no fade and no vignette. A little bare paper in the upper left is fine.
- No ring, no watch, no bracelet, no toy, nothing held in the hands.
