THE PICTURE: a painting for a website's Science page, for the part that introduces the company's chief scientist, who has studied mitochondria for decades. His real portrait (a man in a dark suit) will be placed into the painting afterwards, sitting at the edge of one huge living cell, the thing he studies. So the painting has NO person in it. It must be a real painting someone would frame, wet and loose, never a schoolbook diagram. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: ONE huge cell, so large that only its left part fits in the picture: it runs off the top, the right and the bottom edges.
- The cell's soft, uneven, rounded edge enters at the top edge at about 46% across, bulges left to about 34% across at mid-height, and leaves at the bottom edge at about 44% across. Left of that edge: bare paper, completely empty, from the left edge of the picture (large dark words will sit there).
- Inside the cell: loose wet washes, very pale blue-grey running into sage and a little warm sand, with blooms and back-runs where the paper was wet; palest near its left edge, a little deeper toward the right. The edge itself is the wash's own soft bleeding rim with two or three dry grey ink strokes along it, not a drawn outline.
- The nucleus: ONE large deep blue-grey wet-in-wet form at the far right, centred at about 88% across and 42% down, about 38% of the picture's height, partly cut off by the right edge, darker on one side, its edge bleeding softly into the wash.
- The mitochondria: about twenty-five SLENDER gold marks drifting through the cell like willow leaves on still water, most of them between 60% and 98% across. Each is one single short brush stroke of flat, matte, warm gold, like a sliver of gold leaf laid on the paper: long and thin (about five times longer than wide), gently curved, of different lengths, pointing every which way; a few big ones (as long as a tenth of the picture's width), most of middle size, a few small pale ones. Several gather loosely round the nucleus. No ink outline round them, no lines inside them.
- The band from 36% to 58% across, inside the cell, stays calm and pale, with only three or four small gold slivers in it (the man will be placed in front of it; nothing dark or busy may sit behind his head).

THE GOLD: for this order only, the rule "no gold paint" is lifted for these marks and nowhere else. Flat and matte, like gold leaf: no glow, no shine, no sparkle, no halo, no yellow light round them.

NEVER: no person; no bean, peanut, sausage or capsule shapes; no neat even spacing; no outlines round the gold; nothing that could read as germs or worms: no heads, tails, hairs, spikes or segments, no small dots in clusters, no red, pink, purple or bright green. No bubbles drawn as rings. No ring or circle drawn round the whole cell, nothing that could read as a planet, a moon, an eye or a halo. No text, labels, pointers, scale bar, microscope or lens.

Paint a NEW picture; the attached painting gives only the way of painting (the ink, the wet washes, the bare paper), not its subject.
