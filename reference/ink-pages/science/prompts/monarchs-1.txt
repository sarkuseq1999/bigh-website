THE PICTURE: a painting for a website's Science page, beside the words "You have about 37 trillion cells, and each one makes its own energy." Later, the butterflies' wings in it will turn gold a few at a time, as the morning sun reaches them: a monarch cannot fly until it has warmed its own flight muscles, and each one warms its own, as each cell makes its own energy. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: the monarch butterfly grove in Pacific Grove, on California's Monterey coast, on a winter morning. A few tall eucalyptus trees with pale, peeling bark and long hanging sickle-shaped leaves; from their branches hang loose clusters of monarch butterflies resting with their wings folded, like bunches of dry leaves; and about twenty single butterflies fly in the open air between the trees, wings open. Through the trees at the back, a pale blue-grey glimpse of Monterey Bay.

THE BUTTERFLIES: about 60 to 100 butterflies in all, near ones bigger, far ones small, and every one clearly a butterfly: two pairs of wings, each drawn with a few dark ink veins and a dark ink edge, and a thin dark body. Every butterfly is still in the cold morning shade: its wings are bare white paper or the palest cream inside their ink veins. No orange, no yellow, no gold anywhere yet. Never one solid blob of butterflies: each one can be told apart.

COMPOSITION:
- The trees stand across the full width, the trunks soft and pale, the branches running off the top, left and right edges.
- The butterflies are spread over the middle of the picture, from about 20% to 80% down: the clusters on the branches, the flying ones in the gaps between.
- Lots of bare paper between the branches; the sky is bare paper.
- Below about 85% down: bare paper; the trunks fade out softly at their feet; no hard edge.
- No people, no paths, no fences, no signs, no sun disc, no birds.
