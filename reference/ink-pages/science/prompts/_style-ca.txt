STYLE (every Science painting since November 4, 2026): An ink-and-wash painting on plain warm white paper, by the same painter as the attached painting of the Big Sur coast: take only its way of painting, not its subject. The scene is first drawn and shaded with a brush in grey and black ink, quickly and loosely; then a few light, transparent watercolour washes are laid over it: dry-grass gold, sage, a soft blue-grey, a few dabs of poppy orange, a pale warm light. The ink does the work; the colour is light and calm. Lots of bare paper. You can see every brush stroke; soft bleeding edges where the paper was wet, crisp ones where it was dry. The more it looks like a painting, the better. Not photo-real, no fine detail everywhere, no smooth digital gradients, no pen outlines, not a storybook illustration, not a cartoon.

It is an American, California scene in the spirit of the California plein-air watercolour painters, NOT an Asian ink painting: no pointed misty peaks, no sea of mist or bands of fog, no pine tree clinging to a rock, no cranes, no pagodas, no paper lanterns, no scrolls, no bamboo, no tea sets, no characters, no seals or stamps.

NEVER: no text, letters or numbers anywhere (no writing on pages, spines, signs or labels), no signature, no seal, no frame or border around the picture, no gold paint, no lab glassware (no flasks, beakers, test tubes, jars or bottles), no pills or capsules, no red or rust clustered dots, no glow, no halo, no people unless the order asks for them.
