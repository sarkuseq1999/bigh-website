TWO PAINTINGS ARE ATTACHED. The first (a close view of a climber's sun-browned hand gripping a rounded edge of warm golden-grey granite, her soft coral sleeve at the left, the dark grey of her trouser knee at the lower left) is the picture to paint again: the same hand and forearm, the same skin, sleeve and rock colours, the same long blue-grey cracks, the same loose, wet way of painting. The second (the Big Sur coast) gives only the way of painting.

THE PICTURE: the first painting again as a TALL picture for a phone's screen. It is a view on the way of a slow zoom on a website's Science page (from inside one living cell, out to this hand, out to the woman climbing a granite wall high above Yosemite Valley). It fills the whole screen with no words on it. Portrait, 1024 x 1536.

THE SCENE: close, from the side. Her bare, sun-browned forearm comes in from the left and rises gently to the right; the rolled cuff of her soft coral sleeve shows clearly at about 20% across (well inside the picture, not at its edge). Her hand grips a small rounded edge in the granite, the back of the hand toward us, the fingers curled over the edge, firm and calm. The granite wall fills the picture above, round and below the hand as soft wet washes of warm pale gold-grey, with long blue-grey crack strokes slanting up to the right, as in the first painting. Out of focus: a little of her coral shirt in the upper left; the dark grey of her trouser knee in one or two broad strokes at the lower left.

HOW THE HAND IS PAINTED: as in the first painting. A warm skin wash left to pool at the wrist and the knuckles, bare paper for the light along the back of her hand and the top of the forearm, a broken ink brush line along the shaded side. The back of her hand is one broad, calm patch of warm skin wash. NO fingernails drawn, NO knuckle creases, NO veins, NO tendons, NO wrinkles, no age spots.

SIZE AND PLACE (important):
- The back of her hand is at about 55% across and 52% down.
- Her hand, from wrist to fingertips, is about 22% of the picture's height and about a third of the picture's width.
- Everything that matters (the sleeve's cuff, the forearm, the hand and its fingertips) lies between 18% and 84% across: a phone shows only the middle of the picture's width.

COMPOSITION:
- Only the forearm, the hand, the edge of the sleeve, the granite and the two out-of-focus corners. No face, no helmet, no rope, no metal gear.
- The washes run off all four edges with no fade and no vignette.
- No ring, no watch, no bracelet, no chalk, no glove, no tape.
