THE PICTURE: an idea for the opening painting of a website's Science page, filling the whole first screen under the large words "Our science starts in the cell." ONE walker on a trail in golden California hills is the main subject. The idea: a body is made of trillions of cells, so the walker (and only the walker) is painted in hundreds of small separate dabs of the brush, like a pointillist figure standing in a loosely washed landscape. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE LAND (painted the usual loose way, in ink and light washes): rolling golden California hills in late morning, tall dry grass, two or three dark valley oaks far back, a pale blue-grey coast range far off. A narrow dirt trail climbs from the bottom edge up and away. A few California poppies near the bottom left.

THE WALKER (painted differently, on purpose): ONE older walker in mid-stride on the trail, seen from behind and well to one side (a three-quarter back view, no face): a sun hat, a light shirt with rolled sleeves, a small daypack, one hand swinging free, the other on a wooden walking stick.
- The walker is built ONLY from small separate touches of the brush, several hundred of them: soft rounded dabs, each about the size of a lentil, set side by side with thin slivers of bare paper between them, like a pointillist painting or a mosaic of petals. NO outline round the figure, NO flat washes inside it, no ink lines.
- The dabs take the colour of what they build: cream for the hat, pale blue-grey for the shirt, sage for the pack, warm grey for the trousers, warm tan for the hands and neck, dark warm grey for the boots. The stick is one plain ink stroke.
- From a step back it reads at once as a person walking. Up close it is plainly made of many small living pieces.
- About one dab in ten is flat, matte, warm GOLD instead of colour, scattered through the whole figure, a little thicker at the head, over the back near the heart, and in the legs.

THE GOLD: for this order only, the rule "no gold paint" is lifted for those dabs and nowhere else. Flat and matte, like gold leaf: no glow, no shine, no sparkle, no halo.

SIZE AND PLACE OF THE WALKER (important): the walker is about 44% of the picture's height. The top of the hat is at about 46% down from the top edge, the feet at about 90% down. The walker stands at about 62% across. Nothing tall reaches into the top 40% of the picture.

COMPOSITION:
- The upper left (from the left edge to about 55% across, from the top to about 50% down) is open sky: bare paper with only the palest warm wash, so that large dark words can sit there. No clouds with hard edges, no sun, no birds.
- The hills run off the left, right and bottom edges with no fade and no vignette.
- Only one person. No dog, no buildings, no fences, no signs.

NEVER: the dabs must not drift away from the figure or dissolve into the air (the walker is whole, not falling apart); no dabs in the sky or the land; no red, rust, pink or purple dabs; nothing that reads as a rash, germs, pixels, sequins or confetti. No text, no labels.

Paint a NEW picture; the attached painting gives only the way of painting the land. Loose, wet, quick; lots of bare paper.
