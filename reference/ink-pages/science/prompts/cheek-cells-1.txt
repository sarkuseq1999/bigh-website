THE PICTURE: a painting for a website's Science page, beside the words "You have about 37 trillion cells, and each one makes its own energy." It is what you see in a school microscope when you look at a few of your own cheek cells. Later, the cells in it will turn gold one at a time, as each cell makes its own energy. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: the round field of view of a microscope, in the middle of the picture: a big soft circle of pale warm light on the paper. Its edge is not a line: a soft, darker ink-grey wash round it fades out into bare paper. No black frame, no ring, no lens, no microscope drawn. Inside the circle, about 15 to 25 flat cheek cells float, loosely overlapping here and there: each is a large, thin, irregular shape with gently folded edges (like a thin crumpled sheet of paper), drawn with one light ink line and a pale blue-grey wash, with one small round darker nucleus near its middle. Some cells are whole, some run off the edge of the circle.

THE CELLS: every cell is clearly separate, its outline visible. Every cell is still unlit: a pale blue-grey wash and bare paper inside it. No yellow, no gold yet. Never small dots in clusters, never anything that looks like bacteria or germs, never red, pink or purple.

COMPOSITION:
- The circle is centred; its width is about 85% of the picture's height. Left and right of it, bare warm paper.
- No text, no scale bar, no pointer, no labels.
