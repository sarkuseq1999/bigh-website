THE PICTURE: an idea for the opening painting of a website's Science page, filling the whole first screen under the large words "Our science starts in the cell." It is the same scene as the page has today (a walker on a trail in golden California hills), painted again with the walker much larger and nearer the middle, so that the walker is plainly the main subject. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: rolling golden California hills in late morning, tall dry grass, a few dark valley oaks, a pale blue-grey coast range far off. A narrow dirt trail climbs from the bottom edge up and away. On the trail, ONE older walker in mid-stride, seen from behind and well to one side (a three-quarter back view, so the line of the cheek and the brim of the hat show, but no face): a sun hat, a light shirt with the sleeves rolled, a small daypack, one hand swinging free, the other on a wooden walking stick. The walker is about 36% of the picture's height and stands at about 62% across, the feet at about 84% down, painted in sure ink strokes and two or three washes: a figure, not a portrait. Tall dry grass in the near foreground, a few stems bending as if in a light wind; a few California poppies near the bottom left. One valley oak stands back on the slope at the far right, smaller than the walker reads.

COMPOSITION:
- The upper left (from the left edge to about 52% across, from the top to about 52% down) is open sky: bare paper with only the palest warm wash, so that large dark words can sit there. No clouds with hard edges, no sun, no birds.
- The hills run off the left, right and bottom edges with no fade and no vignette; the land fills the bottom 45%.
- Only one person. No dog, no buildings, no fences, no signs, no bridges.
