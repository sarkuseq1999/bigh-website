THE PICTURE: a painting for a website's Science page, for the part that introduces the company's chief scientist, Dr. Liu, who has published more than 280 scientific papers. A gentle picture joke on the word "papers": he sends them out into the world as paper planes. Light, warm and a little witty, like a New Yorker illustration in watercolour; dignified, never a cartoon. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE ATTACHED PICTURES:
1. A photograph of Dr. Liu. Paint THIS man: his face, his age, his thin metal-rimmed glasses, his dark hair with its side parting, his warm half-smile. He must be recognisable as the same person.
2. A painted portrait of the same man: it shows how he should look when painted (the loose ink strokes of his dark jacket, the soft watercolour of his face).
3. A painting of the Big Sur coast: take only its way of painting (see STYLE), not its subject.

THE SCENE:
- He stands at the right side of the picture, seen from the knees up, in his dark suit jacket (open), pale blue shirt and blue striped tie. His body faces the left. His head is turned a little toward us, as in the photograph, with the same warm half-smile, his eyes following the plane he has just let go.
- His right arm is stretched out toward the left, the hand open: a white paper plane has just left it. In his left hand, at his side, he holds a few more sheets of white paper.
- From his hand a long, loose flight of paper planes sails away to the left and upward across the sky, getting smaller with distance: about twenty-five planes, simple folded darts, each one or two quick strokes of pale grey ink and a touch of blue-grey shadow on white paper. They are spread out unevenly, in ones and twos, at different tilts, along one long rising curve from his hand (at about 62% across, 45% down) to the top left corner (about 6% across, 10% down).
- The sky is bare paper with two or three very pale blue-grey washes, nothing more. Under his feet, a few strokes of dry golden grass and two small poppy-orange dabs, fading into bare paper.

THE GOLD: for this order only, the rule "no gold paint" is lifted for ONE thing: three of the paper planes (one near his hand, two farther off) are flat, matte, warm gold, like planes folded from gold leaf. No glow, no shine, no sparkle, no halo. No gold anywhere else.

COMPOSITION:
- The lower left of the picture (from the left edge to 50% across, from 45% down to the bottom) stays bare paper, completely empty (large dark words will sit there). The planes fly ABOVE that area.
- His head sits at about 76% across and 26% down. Plain bare paper behind his head.
- No horizon line, no hills, no buildings, no trees, no window, no clouds with hard edges.

NEVER: no writing, letters or numbers on any sheet or plane; no birds; no second person; no lab coat; no childish or comic faces, no motion lines, no stars. Not a photograph, not a smooth digital portrait: the same loose ink and watercolour as the rest, his face a little more careful than the rest so that he is recognisable.

Paint a NEW picture.
