TWO PAINTINGS ARE ATTACHED. The first (a woman in her sixties climbing a sunlit granite wall high above Yosemite Valley, Half Dome across the valley) is the scene this picture belongs to: paint the same climber's hand, her same soft coral shirt and sun-browned skin, the same warm golden-grey granite with its blue-grey cracks, in the same clear morning light. The second (the Big Sur coast) gives only the way of painting.

THE PICTURE: a view on the way of a slow zoom on a website's Science page. The zoom starts inside one living cell, pulls back to this picture, then pulls back again to the first attached painting. So this is that painting seen very close: only her lower hand (the one at the height of her chest, gripping the rock) and its forearm. It fills the whole window with no words on it. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: close, from the side, as in the first painting. Her bare, sun-browned forearm comes in from the left edge a little below the middle and rises gently to the right; the rolled cuff of her soft coral sleeve shows at the left edge. Her hand grips a small rounded edge in the granite at the middle of the picture, the back of the hand toward us, the fingers curled over the edge, firm and calm. Behind and round the hand, the warm pale golden-grey granite as soft wet washes, with two or three long blue-grey crack strokes slanting up to the right, as in the first painting. Out of focus: in the upper left corner a little of her coral shirt; in the lower left corner the dark grey of her trouser knee in one or two broad strokes.

HOW THE HAND IS PAINTED: loosely, in a few sure strokes. One broken ink brush line along the shaded side of the forearm and the hand; a warm skin wash left to pool at the wrist and the knuckles; bare paper for the light along the back of her hand and the top of the forearm. The back of her hand is one broad, calm patch of warm skin wash, the largest plain shape in the picture. NO fingernails drawn, NO knuckle creases, NO veins, NO tendons, NO wrinkles, no age spots: the fingers end in simple soft strokes. It reads at once as a strong hand holding on to warm rock, and it is plainly a painting.

SIZE AND PLACE (important): the back of her hand is at the middle of the picture (about 50% across, 50% down). Her hand, from wrist to fingertips, is about 34% of the picture's height. The forearm runs from the left edge (at about 62% down) up to the wrist (at about 54% down). Everything that matters lies between 20% and 80% across.

COMPOSITION:
- Only the forearm, the hand, the edge of the sleeve, the granite and the two out-of-focus corners. No face, no helmet, no rope, no metal gear.
- The washes run off all four edges with no fade and no vignette.
- No ring, no watch, no bracelet, no chalk, no glove, no tape.
