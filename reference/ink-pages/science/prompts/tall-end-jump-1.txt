TWO PAINTINGS ARE ATTACHED. The first (a grandmother cannonballing off a wooden dock into the clear water of Lake Tahoe, two grandchildren cheering on the dock) is the picture to paint again: the same people, the same clothes and colours, the same place, light and loose hand. The second (the Big Sur coast) gives only the way of painting.

THE PICTURE: the first painting again as a TALL picture for a phone's screen, where the words "Why start with the cell? Everything you do runs on energy your cells make." stand in its sky. Portrait, 1024 x 1536.

SIZE AND PLACE (important):
- The top 44% of the picture is open sky: bare paper with only the palest warm wash, nothing in it, so that large dark words can sit there.
- The far shore, pines and ridges lie along about 52% to 58% down; the lake fills the picture below.
- The grandmother, curled up in mid-air, is about 16% of the picture's height, at about 30% to 52% across, 48% to 64% down. The dock comes in from the bottom right to about 50% across; the two children stand on it at about 56% to 78% across. Nothing that matters is in the outer 15% at the left or the right.

Faces in two or three loose strokes only, not storybook faces. Paint a NEW picture. Loose, quick ink brush strokes, light washes, lots of bare paper.
