THE IDEA: the rower at sunrise. A still California lake at first light among low golden hills dotted with dark valley oaks. A fit man in his late sixties rows a long, narrow racing single scull across glassy water, mid-stroke: his body leaning back, arms drawn in, both blades just leaving the water. He wears a pale cap and a light top. Behind the boat a long, thin V-shaped wake spreads out across the lake, and along it, every few metres, the pairs of round ripples his blades left at each stroke, growing wider and fainter the farther back they are. Calm, rhythm, quiet strength. Clear air, no fog, no mist.

THE GOLD (important): the low sun (out of the picture, to the right) lights the ripples. Paint ONLY the ripple rings and the wake lines as thin strokes of flat, even, pale warm ochre-yellow (they will be laid in gold leaf later); everything else in normal colour. No other yellow on the water.

SIZE AND PLACE: the boat and rower are at about 62% to 86% across, the rower's body at about 48% to 62% down, the hull a long thin line at about 62% down. The wake and the pairs of ripple rings trail away behind the boat to the left and slightly up, toward about 15% across, 58% down. The far shore with its golden hills and oaks lies along about 48% to 54% down. The lake fills the bottom half: soft blue-grey and pale warm reflections.

COLOUR: soft blue-grey water with warm reflections, golden hills, dark sage oaks, the rower's top a pale coral; the ochre ripples the brightest warm note.

Paint a NEW picture; the attached painting gives only the way of painting. Loose, quick ink brush strokes, light washes, lots of bare paper. Not an Asian ink painting: no misty mountains, no fisherman's boat, no reeds in mist; a modern racing shell with long oars, on an American lake.
