THE SCENE: late afternoon at Altamont Pass, California: rolling golden hills, bare of trees, with rows of tall white wind turbines standing along every ridge, near and far, the way they do beside the highway east of San Francisco Bay. Each turbine is a slender white tower with three long thin blades, each turned a little differently, painted in a few sure thin ink strokes and a pale grey wash. Three large turbines stand on the nearer ridge at the right; behind them, smaller and smaller ones march along the ridges into the hazy distance, perhaps twenty in all. On a narrow dirt trail on the near hill, the grandfather and the child stand hand in hand, seen from behind and a little to one side, looking out at the turbines; the child's free arm is raised, pointing at the nearest one. Their joined hands are plain to see between them, low, at the middle of the pair.

The idea of the picture: a whole countryside of small power makers, each one making its own energy, as each of our cells does.

SIZE AND PLACE (important): the grandfather is about 40% of the picture's height; the top of his hat at about 48% down from the top edge, his feet at about 88% down. He stands at about 40% across, the child at his right at about 48% across. Their joined hands at about 44% across, 68% down. The three near turbines stand between 62% and 96% across; the tallest one's hub is at about 22% down, its blades reaching up to about 8% down. The far turbines are small, along ridges between 40% and 58% down.

COMPOSITION:
- The upper left (from the left edge to about 56% across, from the top to about 46% down) is open sky: bare paper with only the palest warm wash, so that large dark words can sit there. No turbine, blade, cloud, sun or bird in it.
- The hills run off the left, right and bottom edges with no fade and no vignette; the land fills the bottom 50%.
- Only these two people. No dog, no buildings, no power lines, no fences, no roads with cars, no signs.
- The grass a rich dry gold; the turbines pale and calm, never darker than the people; the child's shirt the one note of soft blue.
