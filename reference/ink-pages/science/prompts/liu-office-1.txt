THE PICTURE: a painting for a website's Science page, for the part that introduces the company's chief scientist, a university professor who studies mitochondria. His real portrait (a man in a dark suit, leaning forward with his hands clasped) will be placed into the painting afterwards, as if he sat behind the desk during office hours. So the painting has NO person in it: an empty place is kept for him. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

THE SCENE: the corner of a professor's office in an old California university, seen straight from the front at seated eye level, warm and quiet.
- A plain old wooden desk runs across the whole lower part of the picture, seen from the front and a little from above. Its far edge is level at about 70% down the picture, its front edge is one straight level line at about 82% down, and below that we see the desk's plain wooden front, which fades out into bare paper before the bottom edge. Warm ochre and soft brown washes over quick ink strokes; the wood's grain is a few dry brush strokes, no more.
- The desk's top is EMPTY from the left edge to 48% across (his hands will rest there). On its right part (from 62% to 92% across): a short stack of three old bound journals lying flat, with blank cloth spines (sage, blue-grey, ochre), and an enamel camp mug holding two pencils. Nothing else on the desk.
- The wall behind is bare paper with the palest warm wash. From the left edge to 44% across the wall is completely plain and pale, from the top of the picture down to the desk: nothing there at all (he will sit there).
- On the wall to the right, an old school blackboard in a wooden frame: its left edge at about 42% across, its right edge at about 95% across, its top at about 9% down, its bottom at about 60% down. The slate is a pale, uneven wash of soft grey-green, much lighter than a real blackboard, with wet blooms and wiped chalk-dust smudges, so it stays airy and never becomes a heavy dark slab. A chalk ledge under it with a piece of chalk and a felt eraser.
- Drawn on the blackboard in loose white chalk, large, centred at about 70% across and 34% down, about 30% of the picture's width: ONE mitochondrion, drawn the way a professor sketches it for students in ten seconds: a long rounded oval outline lying on its side, a second line just inside it, and inside that six or seven deep wavy folds reaching in from the edge. Quick, confident, a little wobbly chalk lines, not a neat diagram.
- Warm morning light from a window we cannot see, falling from the right.

THE GOLD: for this order only, the rule "no gold paint" is lifted for ONE thing: the wavy folds inside the chalk mitochondrion are drawn in flat, matte, warm gold, like gold leaf rubbed onto the slate. No glow, no shine, no sparkle, no halo. The outline stays white chalk. No gold anywhere else.

NEVER: no person, no chair back showing above the desk, no portrait or picture frames on the wall, no window in view, no computer, no lamp, no clock, no globe, no plant, no flowers, no apple, no glasses, no lab equipment of any kind. No writing, letters, numbers, formulas, arrows or labels on the blackboard or anywhere: only the one drawing. Nothing that reads as a child's classroom or a cartoon.

Paint a NEW picture; the attached painting gives only the way of painting (the quick ink, the light washes, the bare paper), not its subject.
