TWO PAINTINGS ARE ATTACHED. The first (a woman climbing a sunlit granite wall high above Yosemite Valley, Half Dome across the valley) is the picture to paint again: the same climber, the same clothes and colours, the same place, light and loose hand. The second (the Big Sur coast) gives only the way of painting.

THE PICTURE: the first painting again as a TALL picture for a phone's screen, where the words "Why start with the cell? Everything you do runs on energy your cells make." stand in its sky. Portrait, 1024 x 1536.

SIZE AND PLACE (important):
- The top 42% of the picture is open sky at the left and middle: bare paper with only the palest warm wash, so that large dark words can sit there. The granite wall may rise along the right edge only (right of 74% across) in that top part.
- The wall fills the right of the picture below that, from about 50% across to the right edge. The climber is about 30% of the picture's height, on the wall's left edge at about 46% to 72% across, 50% to 80% down, her reaching hand high on the rock at about 64% across, 50% down. Nothing that matters is in the outer 15% at the left or the right.
- Half Dome small and pale across the valley at about 24% across, its top at about 62% down; the valley floor, meadow, river and small pines along the bottom fifth, far below.

Clear and sunny, no fog, no cloud bands. Paint a NEW picture. Loose, quick ink brush strokes, light washes, lots of bare paper.
