Two attached pictures. The FIRST (a flying crane) is the style to copy exactly: loose sumi-e brushwork on warm plain paper, a few confident strokes, wide pale grey washes that bleed softly into the paper, rich black only in a few accents laid in one stroke, dry-brush edges with flying white, and a great deal of bare paper; nothing is drawn, outlined or detailed. The SECOND (an open-fronted powerhouse) gives the subject and composition. Paint that subject again in the crane's hand, simpler and looser: a small old powerhouse with its front wall left open like a storybook cutaway, its pitched roof and two side walls a few pale grey washes (grey ink, not pink brick), one short brick chimney on the roof. Inside, left to right, only four things, large and readable at a glance: a worker (small, seen from behind, cap and braces) swinging a split log into the round open door of a plump boiler-furnace whose fire is real gold leaf; a puff of white steam rising from the boiler into one simple pipe that runs right; a big spoked wheel turning, with a few curved motion strokes round its rim; and from the wheel two thin wires leaving the building to a wooden pole at the right, with a fine thread of real gold along the first span of wire. The only gold is the fire and that thread. A small pile of split logs by the worker. Far fewer pipes, bolts, stands and small parts than the reference. The powerhouse fills about three quarters of the picture's width, centred, standing on one soft grey ground wash. Generous empty paper above and round it; the edges dissolve into the paper. It must look painted with a brush, never drawn: no pen outlines, no hard edges, no fine detail, no technical diagram, no photo look. Calm and quiet. Landscape format, 4:3. No text, letters, numbers, arrows, signs or labels anywhere; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame; no Asian cultural motifs; no dark smoke or pollution. Nothing red, no pink or rust-coloured brick; no red, orange or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
