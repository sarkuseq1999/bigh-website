The attached picture (a flying crane) is the style to copy exactly: loose sumi-e brushwork on warm plain paper, a few confident strokes, wide pale grey washes that bleed softly into the paper, rich black only in a few accents laid in one stroke, dry-brush edges with flying white, and a great deal of bare paper; nothing is drawn, outlined or detailed. Do not paint the crane; paint only this subject in its hand: a small historic brick steam power station from about 1905, like the old powerhouses of California towns, standing beside a quiet river at the foot of golden California hills with a few valley oaks. A plain long hall with a pitched roof and four tall arched windows, and one tall, slender round smokestack. Through the arched windows the furnace light shows as real gold leaf, the only gold in the picture: the energy it makes. A worker, small and seen from behind, carries an armful of split logs from a woodpile in through the open door (fuel going in). Three wooden power poles carry thin wires away from the building up over the hill (power going out). A thin plume of pale white steam rises from the smokestack. The building fills about half the picture's width, right of centre; the hills are one or two pale golden-ochre washes; the river one pale grey wash with bare paper. Only a thin, transparent watercolour tint where named. Generous empty paper on every side; the edges dissolve into the paper. Calm and quiet. It must look painted with a brush, never drawn: no pen outlines, no hard edges, no fine detail, no photo look. Landscape format, 4:3. No text, letters, numbers, signs or lettering anywhere, not on the building either; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame; no Asian cultural motifs; no dark smoke or pollution (any steam is pale and light). Nothing red; no red, orange or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
