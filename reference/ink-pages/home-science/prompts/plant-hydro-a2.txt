The attached picture (a flying crane) is the style to copy exactly: loose sumi-e brushwork on warm plain paper, a few confident strokes, wide pale grey washes that bleed softly into the paper, rich black only in a few accents laid in one stroke, dry-brush edges with flying white, and a great deal of bare paper; nothing is drawn, outlined or detailed. Do not paint the crane; paint only this subject in its hand: a small historic stone hydroelectric powerhouse at the foot of a waterfall in a granite canyon of the Sierra Nevada, California, like the early powerhouses on the Kern and American rivers. A long steel pipe runs down the granite slope from the top of the waterfall into the back of the building (water going in). The waterfall and the river below are bare paper with soft white spray and a few pale grey washes; the granite is a few broad grey strokes; a few pines in pale grey-green washes. The building is simple: grey stone walls, a pitched roof, three tall windows whose light is real gold leaf, the only gold in the picture: the energy it makes. Wooden power poles carry thin wires away across the river (power going out). No smoke. The powerhouse fills about a third of the picture's width, in the lower middle. Only a thin, transparent watercolour tint where named. Generous empty paper on every side; the edges dissolve into the paper. Calm and quiet. It must look painted with a brush, never drawn: no pen outlines, no hard edges, no fine detail, no photo look. Landscape format, 4:3. No text, letters, numbers, signs or lettering anywhere, not on the building either; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame; no Asian cultural motifs; no dark smoke or pollution (any steam is pale and light). Nothing red; no red, orange or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
