Now a closer view of that furnace and its chimney, both left open down the front like the building, like a storybook cutaway. The furnace sits low at the left with the worker adding a log at its round door; its fire is real gold leaf. From the furnace a tall square brick chimney (grey ink, not pink) rises straight up through the roof, taking up the middle of the picture from the furnace to near the top, its front open so we see inside it: big sparks fly up it from the fire, about twelve, each a bold flick of black ink as long as a thumb in this picture with a bright head of real gold leaf, swirling upward. At the top of the chimney a rounded wire-mesh spark screen, a pale dome of a few crisscross dry-brush lines, catches most of the sparks, which press against it and show clearly through it; only two sparks have escaped, flying up and away above it into the empty paper. The sparks must be easy to see even when the picture is small. The gold is only in the fire and the sparks. A thin plume of pale smoke above the screen. Few strokes, no small parts. Generous empty paper on the right and above; the edges dissolve into the paper. It must look painted with a brush, never drawn: no pen outlines, no hard edges, no fine detail, no technical diagram, no photo look. Landscape format, 4:3.
