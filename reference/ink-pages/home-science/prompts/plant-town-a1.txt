The attached picture (a flying crane) is the style to copy exactly: loose sumi-e brushwork on warm plain paper, a few confident strokes, wide pale grey washes that bleed softly into the paper, rich black only in a few accents laid in one stroke, dry-brush edges with flying white, and a great deal of bare paper; nothing is drawn, outlined or detailed. Do not paint the crane; paint only this subject in its hand: early evening in a small California valley town about 1910. On the left, a small brick power station with one tall slender smokestack and tall arched windows whose light is real gold leaf; a thin plume of pale steam. From it, a line of wooden power poles with thin wires runs to the right across a pale field into a cluster of small wooden houses and a little church under a few valley oaks, and the windows of the houses and two street lamps glow with small touches of the same real gold leaf: the energy made in the power station reaching every house. The only gold in the picture is that light. The sky is bare paper with one very pale grey wash low down; golden hills far behind in one pale ochre wash. The houses are a few simple strokes each. Only a thin, transparent watercolour tint where named. Generous empty paper on every side; the edges dissolve into the paper. Calm and quiet. It must look painted with a brush, never drawn: no pen outlines, no hard edges, no fine detail, no photo look. Landscape format, 4:3. No text, letters, numbers, signs or lettering anywhere, not on the building either; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame; no Asian cultural motifs; no dark smoke or pollution (any steam is pale and light). Nothing red; no red, orange or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
