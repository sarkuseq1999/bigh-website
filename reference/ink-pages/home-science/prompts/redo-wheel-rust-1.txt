The FIRST attached painting is the companion picture of this one: paint the very same object, by the same painter in the same colours, so the two belong together as a pair. (The second attached painting, a mailbox, is only the brush-hand reference.)

STYLE: A small, quiet watercolour for a California brand's website, in loose thin watercolour on plain white paper. Use the attached painting (a country mailbox) only as the reference for the brush hand (do not paint what it shows): the same painter, the same few wet washes with soft bleeding edges, a few dry-brush streaks, very few strokes, lots of bare white paper. The more it looks like a real watercolour painting, the better. Not photo-real, not a detailed drawing, not a storybook or children's-book illustration, not a cartoon, no pen outlines, no flat cut-out shapes.

It is an American, California subject, in the spirit of the California plein-air watercolour painters, NOT an Asian ink painting: no characters, no seal or stamp.

PALETTE: soft blue-grey, a warm weathered sienna, a little sage green and dry-grass straw, all thin and pale, on pure white paper. The parts named below as gold are real gold leaf: flat and matte, a warm deep gold, not yellow, not glowing.

COMPOSITION: Landscape, 1536 x 1024. One subject, centred, filling about two thirds of the frame, with clear bare white paper on every side. Its edges and the ground under it dissolve softly into the paper; nothing is cut off by the frame; no frame, no border, no background scene, no sky wash. The ground is only a loose pale wash.

NEVER: no people, no hands, no text, letters, numbers or writing anywhere, no signature, no red, no orange flames, no glow, no halo, no sparkle, no lens flare.

HOW LOOSE: paint it in just a few wet washes. Big soft shapes that bleed at the edge, nothing outlined, no fine detail, no rivets, speckle or wood grain. If a part can be left to bare paper, leave it. From across a room, and small on a phone, it must still read at once.

THE SUBJECT: The same wooden water wheel as in the first attached painting, now seen much closer: the centre of the wheel fills the frame, its big iron hub and axle with the inner ends of the wooden spokes around it (the rim dissolves into the paper). Clear pale blue water runs down past it in a few loose wet strokes, and a spray of small blue drops flies off onto the iron hub. The iron hub and axle are soft blue-grey washes, but where the drops have landed, rust has bloomed: four or five soft-edged blooms of deep brown ink and burnt umber eating into the grey iron, plainly damage, wet-in-wet with bleeding edges. No red. One small part of the hub is still clean grey. No gold in this picture except one thin gold line (the wire) leaving the hub toward the upper right. The idea the picture must tell: most of the water does its work, and the drops that stray leave rust.
