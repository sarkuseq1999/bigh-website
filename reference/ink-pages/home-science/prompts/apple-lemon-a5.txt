Use the attached painting ONLY as a style reference: match its loose, wet, confident brushwork and its paper, but do not include its subject. Traditional loose sumi-e ink painting on one plain sheet of warm, light paper, seen flat, evenly lit, no table, no frame. The ink does the painting: each form is made with a few grey ink washes and two or three dark sumi accents, and colour is only a thin, transparent glaze on top (sage green, a warm umber, pale yellow). It must look painted with a brush, never like a pen drawing, a botanical illustration or a tidy watercolour: no outlines, no white highlight rims, no fine detail. Subject: one green apple cut in half, the two halves lying side by side with their cut faces up, and half a Meyer lemon just to the right. The LEFT half has turned brown: one warm umber wash soaking in from its edges over the flesh, like a cut apple left out on the counter (one soft wash, never spots or speckles). The RIGHT half is still fresh: its flesh is bare paper, and one clear drop of lemon juice hangs just above it. The lemon's cut face carries a little real gold leaf, the only gold in the picture. A few quick black marks for seeds. A soft grey ground wash under the fruit. The group fills about sixty percent of the picture's width, centred, with generous empty paper on every side and nothing touching the edges. Landscape picture, 3:2. No knife, no plate, no glass, no hands. No text, letters or numbers; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no red anywhere; no spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
