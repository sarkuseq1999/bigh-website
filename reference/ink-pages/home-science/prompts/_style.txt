Style for every painting in the homepage's "Make sense of the science." section (October 19, 2026).
Each topic gets its own everyday California picture instead of the mitochondrion, which the
"Tiny power plants." section above already shows (Mo, October 19: idea A).

A loose, museum-quality ink-wash brush painting on plain warm white paper (#F6F3EC), in the hand of
a master brush painter, like a sumi-e study: confident, fluid strokes, wet edges bleeding softly into
the paper fibres, dry-brush texture, transparent grey washes and rich black ink. Only a thin,
transparent watercolour tint over the ink (sage green, soft pink or pale yellow where named). It must
look like a painting, never a photo and never a detailed drawing: no fine pen outlines, no
cross-hatching, no shading lines. Generous empty paper margins all round. Landscape format, 3:2.
No text, letters, numbers or labels; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame;
no Asian cultural motifs. Nothing red; no red or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
Exactly one small area of real gold leaf in the picture, where it is named.
