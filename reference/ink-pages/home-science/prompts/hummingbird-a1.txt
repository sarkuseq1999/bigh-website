A loose, museum-quality ink-wash brush painting on plain warm white paper (#F6F3EC), in the hand of a master brush painter, like a sumi-e study: confident, fluid strokes, wet edges bleeding softly into the paper fibres, dry-brush texture, transparent grey washes and rich black ink, with only a thin, transparent watercolour tint of sage green and soft pink. Subject: a small California hummingbird hovering in mid-air beside a tall stem of native California hummingbird sage. The sage stem rises from the lower left in a few quick sage-green brush strokes, with soft pink-magenta flower whorls painted as loose wet dabs. The hummingbird sits right of centre, facing the flowers, its long thin beak almost touching a blossom; its body is a few soft grey-green washes with black ink accents; its throat is soft grey, not red. Its wings are a fast blur: a few sweeping, translucent dry-brush arcs fanning above its back, and those wing arcs are touched with real gold leaf, the only gold in the picture, as if the energy of its flight were gold. Feeling: tireless, light, alive. Generous empty paper all round; bird and flower together fill about two thirds of the frame. Landscape format, 3:2. It must look like a painting, never a photo and never a detailed drawing: no fine pen outlines, no cross-hatching. No text, letters, numbers or labels; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame; no Asian cultural motifs. Nothing red; no red or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
