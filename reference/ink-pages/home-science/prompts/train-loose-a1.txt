Two attached pictures. The FIRST (a flying crane) is the style to copy exactly: loose sumi-e brushwork on warm plain paper, a few confident strokes, wide pale grey washes that bleed softly into the paper, rich black accents laid in one stroke, dry-brush edges with flying white, and a great deal of bare paper; nothing is drawn, outlined or detailed. The SECOND (a small steam engine in a redwood grove) gives only the subject and composition. Paint that subject in the crane's hand: the same small old narrow-gauge steam engine at the same angle and place, but made of very few brush strokes: the boiler one or two pale grey washes with a darker wet edge, the cab a few strokes, the wheels quick circles of dry brush, the tall flared balloon smokestack two strokes, with its rounded mesh spark screen on top as a few light crisscross dry-brush lines. Leave out rivets, pipes, bolts, rods and small parts; much of the engine is pale grey wash and bare paper, with black only in a few accents (the cab roof, the smokestack's rim, the wheels' shadows). The firebox door at the back of the cab is open and the fire inside is a small patch of real gold leaf, the only gold in the picture. A thin plume of pale grey steam. Behind it, the redwoods are just two or three very pale vertical washes; at the front, a few quick strokes of fern in a thin sage green; the track is one dry-brush line. Generous empty paper on every side; the edges dissolve into the paper. Calm and quiet. It must look painted with a brush, never drawn: no pen outlines, no hard edges, no fine detail, no photo look. Landscape format, 4:3. No text, letters, numbers or labels; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame; no Asian cultural motifs. Nothing red; no red, orange or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
