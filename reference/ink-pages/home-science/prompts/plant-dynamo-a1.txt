The attached picture (a flying crane) is the style to copy exactly: loose sumi-e brushwork on warm plain paper, a few confident strokes, wide pale grey washes that bleed softly into the paper, rich black only in a few accents laid in one stroke, dry-brush edges with flying white, and a great deal of bare paper; nothing is drawn, outlined or detailed. Do not paint the crane; paint only this subject in its hand: one antique dynamo, the heart of an old power plant, painted large as a single object on bare paper, seen from the side at three-quarters: a heavy cast-iron base and two big rounded iron field magnets in a few rich black and grey washes; between them the drum is wound with copper coils, and those coils are real gold leaf, the only gold in the picture: the energy it makes. A broad leather belt runs from its pulley out of the picture at the left (the power coming in), and two thin wires curve away from it to the right (the power going out). A few quick curved strokes suggest the drum spinning. Nothing else in the picture; one soft grey ground wash under the base. The machine fills about half the picture, centred. Simple masses, few strokes, no small screws or parts. Only a thin, transparent watercolour tint where named. Generous empty paper on every side; the edges dissolve into the paper. Calm and quiet. It must look painted with a brush, never drawn: no pen outlines, no hard edges, no fine detail, no technical diagram, no photo look. Landscape format, 4:3. No text, letters, numbers, arrows, signs or labels anywhere; no seal, stamp, signature or calligraphy; no border or frame; no Asian cultural motifs; no dark smoke or pollution. Nothing red; no red, orange or rust spots, dots or speckles; no glowing halo.
