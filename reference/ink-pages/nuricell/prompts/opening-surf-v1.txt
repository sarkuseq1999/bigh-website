A traditional ink-wash painting (sumi-e) on one plain sheet of warm, light rice paper with fine fibres, evenly lit, no frame, no text, no signature, no red seal, no stamp. Paint it the way a master ink painter works: a few confident, loose brush strokes, wet ink bleeding softly into the paper, dry-brush texture where the brush runs out, simplified forms that suggest rather than describe, lots of white paper. It must look like a painting, expressive and artistic, with the brushwork of the reference picture: never a realistic drawing, never a detailed illustration, never a pencil sketch, never a 3D render. Sumi ink only, from deep black to soft grey washes, plus real gold leaf only where stated. Copy only the reference's brushwork, never its subject: no bird, no crane, no reeds, no mountains, no sun, no moon, no mist over hills. A very wide picture, about twice as wide as it is tall.

A quiet California beach in the morning, painted with very few strokes. The Pacific is three or four long, calm horizontal strokes of grey wash across the upper half; small gentle waves are only a few pale broken lines, never a big curling wave. The wet sand is a pale, smooth wash across the lower half. Right of the centre, in the shallow water, an older surfer with short grey hair walks out to sea, seen from behind and a little from the side, carrying a long surfboard under one arm: the figure in a few loose, fast strokes, about a third of the picture's height, the board long and pale with one thin centre stripe of real gold leaf: the only gold in the picture. The left third of the picture is empty, smooth sand in the foreground (something will stand there later): keep it clear. No sun, no birds, no boats, no pier, no cliffs, no palm trees, no other people, no footprints, no text.
