A traditional ink-wash painting (sumi-e) on one plain sheet of warm, light rice paper with fine fibres, evenly lit, no frame, no text, no signature, no red seal, no stamp. Paint it the way a master ink painter works: a few confident, loose brush strokes, wet ink bleeding softly into the paper, dry-brush texture where the brush runs out, simplified forms that suggest rather than describe, lots of white paper. It must look like a painting, expressive and artistic, with the brushwork of the reference picture: never a realistic drawing, never a detailed illustration, never a pencil sketch, never a 3D render. Sumi ink only, from deep black to soft grey washes, plus real gold leaf only where stated. Copy only the reference's brushwork, never its subject: no crane, no reeds, no mountains, no sun. A wide picture, about two and a half times as wide as it is tall.

A tribute to the classic American 'large letter' travel postcards of the 1940s: the four capital letters C E L L, very large, side by side on one baseline, each a bold, wide, slightly three-dimensional block letter with a firm ink outline and a shallow ink-wash side to give it depth. Inside each letter, as if seen through a window shaped like the letter, a tiny ink-wash scene of California life, each filling its letter: inside the C, a grey-haired couple dancing; inside the E, a hiker with a walking stick on an oak-dotted trail; inside the first L, a white lighthouse on a rocky point, its lamp real gold leaf (the only gold in the picture), the sea filling the L's foot; inside the second L, a grey-haired woman standing at an easel painting, filling the L's tall stem, a calm bay with a sailboat filling the L's foot. The letters must read clearly as C, E, L, L from far away: their outlines bold and complete. Every scene stays strictly inside its own letter's outline: nothing crosses an outline, and the paper outside the letters (including the empty space above each L's foot, to the right of its stem) stays completely empty. Plain paper around the letters, nothing else, no other words.
