STYLE: A watercolour painting on plain white paper, in the spirit of the California plein-air watercolour painters: one big wet wash, painted quickly and loosely. You can see every brush stroke; soft bleeding edges where the paper was wet. Not photo-real, no smooth digital gradients, no airbrush, no lens flare, not a greeting-card illustration.

THE ATTACHED PAINTING is the picture this one will be laid over: take its sky's clear cerulean blue, its granulation and its way of painting. Paint ONLY sky.

THE PICTURE: the top-right corner of that same clear California morning sky, where the morning sun is just beyond the top right corner of the paper, out of the picture. Landscape, 1536 x 1024.

- The whole sheet is one big wet wash of the attached painting's clear cerulean blue, uneven as a real wash is, with granulation and brush direction showing.
- In the top right corner the sunlight comes in. There the painter lifted the blue out while it was wet, so the very corner is bare white paper, and a warm light of very pale Naples yellow, with a breath of cadmium yellow light, bleeds out of the corner wet-in-wet into the blue: the blue turns paler and warmer toward the corner.
- The light reaches about 40% of the way across from the right edge and about 55% of the way down from the top, and fades out unevenly into the blue, with soft blooms and one or two backruns where the yellow met the wet blue. Its edge is irregular and painterly: never a smooth circle, never a ring.
- The left half and the bottom of the sheet are plain blue sky wash, like the attached painting's sky.

NEVER: no sun disc, no rays or beams, no sparkle, no lens flare, no clouds, no birds, no land, no sea, no horizon, no text, letters or numbers, no signature, no seal, no frame or border, no gold paint, no orange or red.
