A traditional ink-wash painting (sumi-e) on one plain sheet of warm, light rice paper with fine fibres, evenly lit, no frame, no text, no signature, no red seal, no stamp. Paint it the way a master ink painter works: a few confident, loose brush strokes, wet ink bleeding softly into the paper, dry-brush texture where the brush runs out, simplified forms that suggest rather than describe, lots of white paper. It must look like a painting, expressive and artistic, with the brushwork of the reference picture: never a realistic drawing, never a detailed illustration, never a pencil sketch, never a 3D render. Sumi ink only, from deep black to soft grey washes, plus real gold leaf only where stated. Copy only the reference's brushwork, never its subject: no crane, no reeds, no mountains, no sun. A wide picture, about two and a half times as wide as it is tall.

A tribute to the classic American 'large letter' travel postcards of the 1940s: the four capital letters N U R I, very large, side by side on one baseline, each a bold, wide, flat block letter (a heavy sans-serif capital, like a poster letter) drawn with one firm, slightly rough ink outline: flat, no 3D sides, no shadow, no depth. Inside each letter, as if seen through a window shaped like the letter, a tiny ink-wash scene of California life, each filling its letter: inside the N, a grey-haired surfer riding a small wave; inside the U, a hillside of California poppies whose flowers are real gold leaf (the only gold in the picture); inside the R, a cyclist on a coast road with the sea beside it; inside the I, one tall redwood trunk with ferns at its foot. The letters must read clearly as N, U, R, I from far away: their outlines bold and complete. Every scene stays strictly inside its own letter's outline: nothing crosses an outline, and the paper outside the letters (including the inside of the R's bowl and the space inside the U) stays completely empty. Plain paper around the letters, nothing else, no other words.
