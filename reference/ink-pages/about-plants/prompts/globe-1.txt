COMPOSITION: Landscape, 1536 x 1024, a hand-painted globe of the Earth, like a page from an American naturalist's sketchbook. The LAST attached image is the guide: the light grey disc IS the globe and the darker grey shapes ARE the land on it. Keep the globe's circle and every coastline exactly where they are in that guide: the same position, size and shape (North America with California in the upper left of the globe, South America below, a little of Europe and Africa at the right edge). Geography must stay correct.

Paint the oceans inside the circle in a very pale blue-grey wash, laid wet, a little deeper toward the globe's lower right edge so it reads as round, and fading softly at the globe's edge (no hard outline around the globe). Paint the land in pale grey ink washes, a little darker where the wash pools along the coasts, with a thin pale sage-green or ochre tint here and there. Outside the circle everything is bare paper.

Keep it flat and quiet: no grid or latitude lines, no borders, no cities, no labels, no stars, no space, no clouds, no shadow on the paper, no dots, no plants, no ships.

STYLE: A painting for a California brand's website, in loose ink and thin watercolour on plain warm white paper. Use the attached paintings only as the reference for the brush hand (do not paint what they show): the same painter, the same few wet washes with soft bleeding edges, a few dry-brush streaks, very few strokes, lots of bare paper. The more it looks like a painting, the better. Not photo-real, not a detailed botanical drawing, not a storybook illustration, no pen outlines.

INK FIRST, COLOUR SECOND: paint it mostly in soft grey sumi ink washes, like the attached oak, with only thin, pale touches of the colours named below laid over the ink. Fewer strokes than you think.
