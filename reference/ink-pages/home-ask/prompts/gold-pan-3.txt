STYLE: A small, quiet still life for a California brand's website, in loose ink and thin watercolour on plain warm white paper. Use the attached paintings only as the reference for the brush hand (do not paint what they show): the same painter, the same few wet washes with soft bleeding edges, a few dry-brush streaks, very few strokes, lots of bare paper. The more it looks like a painting, the better. Not photo-real, not a detailed drawing, not a storybook illustration, no pen outlines. Mostly soft grey ink, with only a thin touch of the colours named below.

It is an American, California subject, in the spirit of the California plein-air watercolour painters, NOT an Asian ink painting: no inkstone, no calligraphy brush, no scroll, no pagoda, no bamboo, no twisted pine, no characters, no seal or stamp.

COMPOSITION: Landscape, 1536 x 1024. One subject, centred, filling about two thirds of the frame, with wide clear bare paper on every side. Its edges and the ground under it dissolve softly into the paper; nothing is cut off; no frame, no table edge, no background scene. The ground is only a loose pale wash.

NEVER: no people, no hands, no text, letters, numbers or writing anywhere (not on the object either), no signature, no lab glassware, no pills or capsules, no powder, no red or rust clustered dots, no glow, no halo, no sparkle.

THE SUBJECT: An old prospector's gold pan from the California Gold Rush, tilted up on two river stones in a shallow creek, seen from a three-quarter view so we look down into it, with a miner's short-handled pick lying on the stones beside it, so that it plainly reads as the Gold Rush and not as a kitchen bowl. The pan is two wet washes of soft grey, its rim one darker stroke. A little water in the pan is bare paper with one pale wash. In the crease of the pan just five or six flakes of real gold leaf, flat and matte, the only gold, and no sand or grit. The creek is one pale blue-grey wash fading into the paper.

HOW LOOSE (the painter's client asked for this): paint it in just a few wet washes, the way the attached hummingbird is painted. Big soft shapes that bleed at the edge, nothing outlined, no fine detail, no scratches, rust, rivets, speckle or grain. If a part can be left to bare paper, leave it. From across a room it must still read at once.
