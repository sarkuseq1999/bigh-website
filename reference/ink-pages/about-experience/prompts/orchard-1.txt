COMPOSITION: Square, 1024 x 1024. A California orange grove in Riverside's inland valley on a clear spring morning. The story: one old orange tree came first, and the whole grove behind it grew from it.

- In front, a little left of centre, ONE old orange tree, the largest thing in the picture (about half the picture's width): a short, thick, weathered trunk in warm grey-brown that splits low into a few sturdy limbs, and a broad, rounded, dense crown of sage and olive green leaves, heavy with fruit. It is clearly OLD but healthy and generous: NOT bare, NOT twisted, NOT dying, no dead branches.
- Round its foot, a low, simple old iron fence, about knee high, a few thin warm-grey strokes in a small ring: the kind that marks a tree as special and looked after.
- GOLD LEAF: about twelve to fifteen of the old tree's oranges are small round dabs of flat matte gold leaf, scattered through its crown. This is the only gold in the picture.
- Behind it, to the right and into the distance, rows of YOUNG orange trees: small, round, fresh green dabs in straight rows that run away toward the horizon, getting smaller and paler, each with a few tiny pale orange dots of fruit.
- Far behind: a long line of pale blue-grey mountains, a little snow on the highest tops (the San Gabriel Mountains), under a pale washed sky with lots of bare paper.
- The ground: warm straw and pale ochre earth between the rows, a few dry-brush strokes; a little soft shade under the old tree.

Mood: a sunny, fresh, generous morning. Quiet and calm. No people, no buildings, no houses, no cars, no signs, no birds.
